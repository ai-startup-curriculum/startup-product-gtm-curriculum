# mod-108 — Demand Generation and Channels (One Primary + One Experimental)

**Stage:** SEED → SERIES-A
**Pillar:** GTM
**Hours:** 28 (7 lecture chapters + 6 exercises + 1 lab)
**Track:** [Startup Product & GTM](../../CURRICULUM.md)

> *Most businesses get zero distribution channels to work. If you can get just one to work, you have a great business. If you try for several but don't nail one, you're finished.* — Peter Thiel, *Zero to One*, chapter 11.

## What this module trains

Run the **one-primary-plus-one-experimental** channel doctrine — the operational consensus of Weinberg & Mares's *Traction* Bullseye framework, Brian Balfour's Four-Fit dependency, and Peter Thiel's distribution chapter — instead of the six-channel parade every seed-stage founder defaults to. Pick a **primary channel** funded to 100% of its minimum viable investment and an **experimental channel** funded to exploratory scale, and defer everything else to a dated "not now" list. Diagnose whether the primary is actually working via the **four-gate framework** — ICP-fit lead quality, unit economics (CAC / LTV / payback), time-to-signal maturity, attribution honesty — before scaling spend or headcount into it. Design each of the four archetypes the module develops: **inbound / content + SEO** in the HubSpot inbound methodology (Halligan & Shah) modernised for the Helpful-Content-update and LLM-answered-search era; **outbound at seed scale** in the *Predictable Revenue* (Ross) tradition with the three seed-scale modifications (founder-as-first-SDR, hand-curated list, deep personalisation on every touch); **community** in the four-sub-motion taxonomy (early-adopter cohort / community-led content / DevRel / event-led) grounded in Wenger's *Communities of Practice* and Spinks's SPACES model; and **paid acquisition** at pre-Series-A with spend guardrails, the small-experiment discipline, and the pre-PMF trap (buying a growth curve you cannot retain). Install the **channel-experimentation cadence** — weekly gate reviews, quarterly channel reviews, annual re-derivation — that keeps the portfolio rotating as channels decay (Andrew Chen's *law of shitty click-throughs*), because the founder's job is not to find a magic channel but to keep one working while the next is being seeded.

The failure mode this module exists to catch: **the founder tries to run every channel that has ever worked for anyone, in parallel, at 15-20% of the investment each channel actually requires — and eighteen months later the company has burned two engineers, a full-time marketing hire, and a chunk of the seed round producing no repeatable acquisition channel and no defensible signal about which channel it should be running.** The failure has a mirror: **the founder finds one working primary in year one, treats it as the durable engine of the business, invests everything into scaling it through year two, watches the channel decay in year three, and has no second channel prepared to take over** — because no channel-experimentation cadence was ever installed.

## Learning objectives

- Pick one primary + one experimental demand-generation channel per stage — founder-led outbound, inbound content + SEO, community, paid acquisition, partnerships / channel — and reject the anti-pattern of running six half-funded channels at once.
- Diagnose channel-market fit — does the channel produce ICP-fit leads at a CAC that survives the LTV / payback benchmark? — before scaling spend or headcount into it, using the four-gate framework (ICP-fit, unit economics, time-to-signal, attribution).
- Design inbound / content + SEO for a technical founder — topic strategy, publishing cadence, distribution flywheel, attribution — using the HubSpot inbound methodology as the vocabulary anchor and modern SEO benchmarks (Helpful-Content, topical authority, LLM-cited-search).
- Design outbound at seed scale — the founder as the first SDR, target-account lists, personalised sequences, response-rate expectations, cadence discipline — using *Predictable Revenue* as the operating tradition and the three seed-scale modifications.
- Design a community motion — early-adopter cohort, community-led content, developer relations, event-led — and know why community is the slowest to seed and the strongest to retain.
- Design paid at pre-Series-A — search, social, retargeting — with guardrails on spend, attribution honesty, and awareness of the pre-PMF trap of buying a growth curve you cannot retain.
- Recognise Andrew Chen's *law of shitty click-throughs* — every channel decays as it saturates; the founder job is not to find a magic channel but to run a channel-experimentation cadence with a quarterly review and an annual re-derivation.

## How this module connects to the rest of the track

- **Fed by ← [mod-102](../mod-102-pmf-signal-and-measurement)** — the PMF signal is a hard pre-condition for scaling paid past the small-experiment budget (Chapter 6). A paid programme scaled before the mod-102 "very disappointed" cohort has fired is the classic pre-PMF trap.
- **Fed by ← [mod-103](../mod-103-positioning-and-messaging)** — every outbound message, every inbound headline, every paid ad copy, and every community anchor post draws its language from the positioning statement. Ad copy authored without mod-103 defaults to feature-listing and produces low click-through even at appropriate CPC.
- **Fed by ← [mod-104](../mod-104-icp-and-buyer-personas)** — the ICP is the load-bearing input to every channel decision. Chapter 1's three-question test starts with "where does the ICP already look for solutions?" — the answer routes to the primary channel. Chapter 2's Gate 1 (ICP-fit lead quality) is scored directly against the mod-104 scorecard. Chapter 4's target-account list is built against the mod-104 firmographic + behavioural criteria.
- **Fed by ← [mod-105](../mod-105-founder-led-sales)** — Chapter 1's three-question test's second question is "where has founder-led sales already produced signal?" — the answer is the strongest working hypothesis for the primary channel. Chapter 4's seed-outbound is the direct scale-up of the mod-105 Chapter 2 cold-outreach discipline; the six readiness signals for the founder-to-SDR transition are shared between mod-105 Chapter 8 and mod-108 Chapter 4.
- **Fed by ← [mod-106](../mod-106-pricing-and-packaging)** — the ACV band and pricing metric are hard inputs to Chapter 1's three-question test (CAC math) and Chapter 2's Gate 2 (payback envelope). A pack change (mod-106 Chapter 5) triggers a mandatory channel-market-fit re-check per Chapter 7's cross-module rhythm.
- **Fed by ← [mod-107](../mod-107-sales-motion-design)** — the sales motion decides what shape of leads the channel needs to produce. A PLG motion pairs naturally with inbound + community; an SDR-AE motion pairs with founder-led outbound + targeted paid; an enterprise MEDDPICC motion pairs with partnerships + events. A channel-motion mismatch is the same class of failure as a motion-ICP mismatch (mod-107 Chapter 6).
- **Feeds → [mod-109](../mod-109-retention-expansion-and-growth-loops)** — the channel-of-first-touch cohort tags produced by this module's Chapter 2 attribution discipline are the primary input to mod-109's per-channel retention analysis. The community sub-motion (Chapter 5) is where mod-109's retention-loop and expansion-loop work is often seeded; the early-adopter cohort is both a demand-gen surface and a retention surface.
- **Feeds → [mod-110](../mod-110-gtm-metrics-and-first-hires)** — first-hire decisions (first SDR, first content lead, first paid-marketing hire) are downstream of the mod-108 quarterly review's portfolio-stability read. A hire made against a decaying channel is a hire whose ramp period lands on a diminishing return; the mod-108 review is the input to mod-110's hiring-timing decisions.
- **Feeds → [`project-102-first-scaled-channel`](../../projects/project-102-first-scaled-channel)** *(if authored)* — the channel-portfolio decision and the four-gate diagnosis produced in this module's exercises are the operating instruments the first-scaled-channel project runs on.
