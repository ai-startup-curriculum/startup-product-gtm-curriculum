# The Channel Portfolio — One Primary + One Experimental

## Motivation

Every seed-stage founder eventually wakes up on a Monday morning and writes some version of the same list on the whiteboard: **"we should do content and SEO, and outbound, and we should have a community, and maybe some paid search, and we're going to sponsor that podcast, and partnerships — Jane knows the head of BD at Acme, let's do a co-marketing thing."** By Friday, one person is drafting a blog post, one person is spinning up a Discord, one person is loading a Google Ads account, one person is writing a cold-email cadence, one person is scoping a co-marketing landing page — and none of them is going to finish. Six weeks later the blog has three posts on it, the Discord has 40 members and no messages, the ads have burned $3K without a conversion, the cold cadence is at 0.4% reply rate, and the co-marketing landing page is a Figma file nobody has opened since. The founder writes a Notion doc titled *"why isn't our marketing working?"* and starts the cycle again.

This is not a marketing problem. It is a **portfolio-discipline problem**. Every channel — content, SEO, outbound, community, paid, partnerships, events, PR, referrals — has a *minimum viable investment level* below which it produces no meaningful signal. Blog posts don't rank on the third one; they rank on the sixtieth. Outbound doesn't work at 40 touches a week; it works at 400. A community isn't a community at 40 lurkers; it's a community when 5% of members post weekly and it has been alive long enough for members to know each other. Paid doesn't reveal its unit economics on $3K of spend; it reveals them at $30K–$50K of consistent spend across enough conversion events to compute a defensible CAC. Six channels each funded at 15% of their minimum viable investment produce zero repeatable-scale channels; one channel funded at 100% of minimum viable investment plus one experimental channel funded at exploratory scale produces one working channel and one hypothesis about the next one.

The failure mode this chapter exists to catch: **the founder tries to run every channel that has ever worked for anyone, in parallel, at a fraction of the investment each channel actually requires — and eighteen months later the company has burned two engineers, a full-time marketing hire, and a large chunk of the seed round producing no repeatable acquisition channel and no defensible signal about which channel it *should* be running.**

The remedy is a doctrine, borrowed loosely from portfolio-management practice: **one primary channel, funded to the level where it can either work or fail visibly, plus one experimental channel, funded at exploratory scale to generate the next hypothesis.** That is it. Everything else is a "not now" — logged, dated, and re-evaluated when the primary channel stabilises or the experimental channel graduates. This chapter builds the doctrine, walks the tradition it comes from, defines the six channel archetypes the module develops, and hands off to Chapter 2 (which develops the *diagnosis* that tells you whether the primary channel is actually working).

## Core concepts

### The tradition — Bullseye, four-fit, and *Zero to One*

The one-primary-plus-one-experimental discipline is not novel — it is the operating consensus of three converging traditions.

**Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares — *Traction* (2015).** *Traction* names nineteen distinct acquisition channels (SEM, SEO, targeting blogs, content marketing, email marketing, viral marketing, engineering as marketing, business development, sales, affiliate programs, existing platforms, trade shows, offline events, speaking engagements, community building, unconventional PR, publicity, targeting local ads, offline ads) and prescribes a discipline they call the **Bullseye Framework**: brainstorm every channel; rank by plausibility; pick three for cheap experiments; **double down on the single channel that shows the strongest early signal** ([Weinberg & Mares 2015 — book site](https://tractionbook.com/)). The Bullseye's core claim is that *one channel dominates at any given stage* — the founder's job is to find that channel, not to run all of them. Bullseye is the closest thing the practitioner literature has to a canonical framework for channel selection at the pre-scale stage.

**Brian Balfour — the Four-Fit framework (Reforge, 2017-2020).** Balfour extends the product-market-fit vocabulary into a four-layer dependency: **Product ↔ Market fit** is necessary but not sufficient; **Market ↔ Channel fit** is the question of whether the target market is reachable at a defensible cost through the chosen channel; **Channel ↔ Model fit** is whether the channel's CAC curve is compatible with the pricing model's LTV curve; **Model ↔ Market fit** closes the loop back to the market. His central claim is that a change to any one layer forces re-validation of the other three — a pricing change can break channel-model fit, an ICP shift can break market-channel fit, and running a channel that does not fit the market or the model is what most stalled growth curves have in common ([Balfour — "The Never Ending Road To PMF" (2017)](https://brianbalfour.com/four-fits-growth-framework), [Reforge — Four Fits](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops)). The four-fit framework is the intellectual backbone of the diagnosis half of this module — it is what Chapter 2 builds against.

**Peter Thiel — *Zero to One* (2014), Chapter 11.** Thiel's line: *"most businesses get zero distribution channels to work: poor sales rather than bad product is the most common cause of failure. If you can get just one distribution channel to work, you have a great business. If you try for several but don't nail one, you're finished"* ([Thiel & Masters 2014](https://zerotoonebook.com/)). Thiel writes from an investor's perch rather than an operator's, but the operational implication is the same as Weinberg's and Balfour's: **the search is for the one channel that works, not for the portfolio that looks diversified.**

Three writers, three different vocabularies, one operating claim: **at the pre-scale stage, the founder's job is to find the single channel that fits the market and the model, and to fund it to the level where it can either work or fail visibly.** The one-primary-plus-one-experimental doctrine is the operational shape of that claim.

### Why "one primary" — the minimum-viable-investment claim

Every channel has a floor beneath which it cannot produce signal. The floor is different for every channel; but for every channel it is real, it is well above zero, and it is well above the level a resource-constrained founder tends to fund each channel at when running six in parallel.

Working floor estimates for the six channels this module covers (calibrated against the practitioner benchmark literature — these are working starting points, not laws; every ICP + product combination shifts them):

| Channel | Minimum viable investment | Time to first signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder-led outbound** | ~15-25 hours/week of founder time; ~400-800 targeted touches/month; a cadence tool ($100-500/month) and enrichment ($200-1000/month) | 4-8 weeks |
| **Inbound / content + SEO** | ~1-2 publishable pieces per week for 6-12 months; ~20-40 hours/week of writer time (founder or hire); an SEO stack ($200-800/month) | 6-12 months |
| **Community** | ~10-15 hours/week of community-manager time (founder or hire) for 6-12 months; a platform (free-to-cheap) plus event/content budget ($500-2000/month) | 6-18 months |
| **Paid acquisition** | ~$5-15K/month sustained spend across at least one channel for 3-6 months; conversion tracking + attribution ($200-500/month) | 6-12 weeks |
| **Partnerships / channel** | 1 dedicated FTE (partnerships lead) or 10-15 hours/week of founder time; joint enablement + co-marketing budget ($5-15K/quarter) | 3-9 months |
| **Events / DevRel** | 1-2 speaking or sponsorship engagements per quarter; ~10-20 hours/week of speaker/organiser time; travel + booth + swag ($5-25K/event) | 3-6 months |

Two facts fall out of the table.

**First, no channel produces signal in less than four weeks, and most take six months to a year.** A founder who runs a channel for four weeks and concludes "it doesn't work" is running an under-invested experiment, not a channel test. The failure is diagnostic-invisible — the channel neither confirms nor rejects the hypothesis because the experiment did not run long enough to matter.

**Second, if you multiply the minimum viable investments together, you get a total commitment that no seed-stage team can sustain across all six channels.** A team funding six channels at 15-20% of minimum viable investment each is running six diagnostic-invisible experiments simultaneously. When the eighteen-month reflection comes and "nothing worked", the founder has no defensible signal about *any* of the six — because none of them was funded to the level where it could have worked.

The one-primary-plus-one-experimental doctrine is the direct implication: **pick one channel and fund it to 100% of its minimum viable investment level for the full time-to-signal window; pick one experimental channel and fund it to exploratory scale (20-40% of minimum viable investment, deliberately time-boxed) to generate the next hypothesis; defer everything else to a "not now" list.**

### The six channel archetypes

The rest of the module develops six channel archetypes. The archetypes are the standard practitioner list (compressing Weinberg's nineteen into the six that dominate B2B SaaS at seed-to-Series-A scale; the collapsed channels — trade shows, unconventional PR, offline ads, targeting local ads, affiliate programs — are covered as sub-plays inside these six, or as "not now" defaults at seed).

- **Founder-led outbound (Chapter 4).** The founder — or a small cohort of founder-adjacent operators — runs personalised outbound sequences against a hand-curated target-account list. Descends from Aaron Ross's *Predictable Revenue* tradition (mod-107 Chapter 3) but operates at seed scale where the founder is the first SDR.
- **Inbound / content + SEO (Chapter 3).** Publish content the ICP searches for or is served by their existing content-consumption graph (search engines, LinkedIn, newsletters, YouTube). Attract → convert → close → delight in the HubSpot inbound methodology's vocabulary.
- **Community (Chapter 5).** Build a group of users, prospective users, and adjacent practitioners who talk to each other about the problem the product solves; the founder participates rather than broadcasts. Slowest to seed; strongest to retain.
- **Paid acquisition (Chapter 6).** Buy attention on search (Google Ads, Bing), social (LinkedIn Ads, Meta, Twitter/X, Reddit), display / retargeting, or a niche network. Fastest to measure; the most dangerous at the pre-PMF stage because it lets you buy a growth curve you cannot retain.
- **Partnerships / channel.** Route to buyers through a company that already has them — an integration partner, a reseller, a systems integrator, a marketplace listing. Slow to negotiate; strong when the partner's distribution economics work.
- **Events / DevRel.** Speak at conferences, sponsor or organise events, run webinars, launch on Product Hunt / Show HN, publish on YouTube channels with technical audiences. Often folded into "community" but has its own operating shape.

Every channel has a matched motion in mod-107 (a PLG motion pairs naturally with community and inbound; an SDR-AE motion pairs naturally with founder-led outbound and paid; an enterprise MEDDPICC motion pairs naturally with partnerships and events). A **channel-motion mismatch** is the same class of failure as a motion-ICP mismatch — the channel produces leads the motion cannot convert.

### How to pick the one primary

The primary channel is not the channel the founder likes best, or the channel her last company used, or the channel currently fashionable on Twitter. It is the channel that scores highest on a three-question test.

**Question 1 — where does the ICP already look for solutions to this problem?** The channel decision is downstream of the mod-104 ICP definition. If the ICP is *"platform engineers at 50-150-engineer B2B SaaS companies"*, they are searching for solutions in Google (documentation and how-to queries), on GitHub (open-source alternatives), on Hacker News and Lobsters (peer discussion), in a handful of engineering-leader newsletters (Kelsey Hightower's writing, Bytes.dev, Engineering Enablement Weekly, etc.), and — increasingly — via LLM-answered queries against those same sources. They are not on LinkedIn Ads at scale, they are not at IT-manager trade shows, they are not reading gated PDFs promoted through Google Ads. The channel decision has to route through where they *actually* look.

If the ICP is *"VP of Marketing at 500-2000-employee mid-market B2B companies"*, the answer is nearly the opposite: they are on LinkedIn (both organic and paid), they read gated content promoted through display and social, they attend MarTech and Ops events, they respond to well-targeted outbound at their role level. The same channel decision routes to a different answer.

**Question 2 — where has the founder-led sales motion (mod-105) already produced signal?** The founder has been personally selling for months by the time this module fires. Where did the first 15-30 deals come from? A conference introduction? A cold email to the founder's LinkedIn network? A tweet that went semi-viral? An HN post? Whichever channel produced the founder-led signal is the strongest working hypothesis for the primary channel — because it has already been tested against the actual ICP with real revenue as the outcome.

The trap here: **founders discount the channel that produced the founder-led deals because it "isn't scalable" or "was luck".** The correct response is: the channel worked at N=1 (the founder herself). The next question is whether it works at N=10 (a small team plus the founder's personal reach) — and if it does, whether it scales. Discounting a channel that has *actually produced revenue* in favour of a channel that has produced zero revenue but is "supposed to work" is a diagnostic error.

**Question 3 — what does the ACV + payback math support?** Chapter 2 develops the diagnosis in full; the short version here: **the CAC of the channel has to survive the payback benchmark that the ACV and gross margin support.** A $12K-ACV mid-market SaaS motion (mod-107's Loomly example) can support a fully-loaded blended CAC of roughly $6-15K (12-24 month payback at 70-80% gross margin); a founder-led outbound channel at that ACV band has to produce leads at a per-closed-customer cost inside that envelope. A $99/month self-serve SMB product can support a CAC of at most $300-800 — which rules out human-touch channels entirely and forces the primary channel into inbound / SEO / community / paid with rigorous CPA discipline.

The three questions produce a ranked list of candidate primary channels. The top-ranked candidate is the primary; the second-ranked or a strategically-different candidate becomes the experimental. The rest go to the "not now" list.

### How to pick the one experimental — and why "experimental" is not "half-hearted"

The experimental channel is not a fallback. It is a **deliberate second-order investment** with three purposes:

1. **Hedge against the primary channel failing.** If the primary channel does not produce channel-market fit in its time-to-signal window (Chapter 2's diagnosis), the experimental channel becomes the next candidate primary. Without a running experimental channel, a failed primary means starting the channel-selection loop from scratch.
2. **Generate the next-stage hypothesis.** Channels decay (Chapter 7). Even a working primary channel eventually reaches a saturation point where the marginal CAC exceeds the LTV / payback envelope. The experimental channel is where the next primary is nucleated *before* the current one saturates.
3. **Cover a strategic gap the primary does not.** A primary channel of founder-led outbound produces high-intent late-funnel leads but nothing top-of-funnel; an experimental channel of inbound content is where the top-of-funnel awareness gets built for the next generation of buyers. The two channels can be *strategically complementary* rather than parallel.

Because the experimental channel has these three jobs, it has to be **funded to exploratory scale (20-40% of minimum viable investment), time-boxed (usually a quarter or two), and instrumented (Chapter 2's diagnosis applies) — but not treated as scale-critical.** It is a real investment, not a decorative one. A "we'll also do some content" line item with no writer, no calendar, no distribution plan, and no attribution instrumentation is not an experimental channel — it is a "not now" pretending to be an experiment.

### Why not two primaries — the fixed-cost claim

The most common objection to the one-primary-plus-one-experimental doctrine is: *"we're a well-funded team, we can run two primaries in parallel."* The objection is almost always wrong at seed-to-Series-A stage, for three reasons.

**First, every channel has a fixed cost of expertise, tooling, and organisational attention that does not diminish when the team is well-funded.** An SDR-AE outbound motion (Chapter 4) needs a target-account list built and maintained, a cadence tool configured, an enrichment stack, a comp plan, a Monday standup, and — most critically — a founder or first sales hire who has the pattern-knowledge to tune the cadence when the response rate drifts. An inbound content motion (Chapter 3) needs a topic calendar, a writer, a publishing rhythm, an SEO instrumentation stack, a distribution flywheel, and — most critically — a founder or first content hire who has the pattern-knowledge to identify which topics compound and which do not. Both motions have irreducible attention costs that are paid whether the team funds them at 100% or 200% of minimum viable investment. Two primaries mean two irreducible attention costs — and at seed-to-Series-A stage, the constraint is nearly always attention, not budget.

**Second, running two primaries in parallel prevents the founder from distinguishing "channel A is working" from "channel B is working" from "the mix is working."** Attribution at seed stage is bad. When leads convert, half of them will not know which of the two channels first exposed them to the product, or will report the wrong one, or will report both. Two primaries in parallel produce two channels' worth of noise and a single blended signal. One primary produces a cleaner attribution story that supports the Chapter 2 diagnosis; the experimental channel is small enough that its noise does not swamp the primary's signal.

**Third, the "two primaries" temptation is usually a symptom of not having done the channel-selection work — the founder does not want to choose because either channel *might* be right.** The remedy is not to run both; the remedy is to pick the one that scores best against the three-question test, run it hard, and hold the second as the experimental. If the pick was wrong, Chapter 2's diagnosis will make it visible inside the time-to-signal window; the primary and experimental swap roles.

The one exception where two primaries can be run in parallel: **a stacked motion where the two channels naturally feed different stages of the funnel.** A PLG-plus-sales-assist motion (mod-107 Chapter 2) may run inbound content as the top-of-funnel primary (generating PQL sign-ups) *and* founder-led outbound as the target-account primary (generating hand-raiser meetings on high-ICP accounts) — because the two channels are producing different lead shapes for different pipeline stages, not competing for the same conversion event. Even in this exception, the founder should be able to name the ACV-tier boundary at which the funnel routes from one channel to the other; if she cannot, it is two parallel primaries pretending to be one stack.

## Concrete example — Loomly picks its Q1 channel portfolio

Loomly (dependency-graph SaaS for B2B engineering teams; ICP = 50-150-engineer B2B SaaS companies on GitHub; ACV = $12-24K; motion = SDR-AE inside sales with a PLG top-of-funnel layer, per mod-107 Chapter 1) sits down at the start of Q1 to pick its channel portfolio. Jane, the founder, has been running founder-led sales for two quarters and has closed 22 mid-market deals (per mod-105). She just hired her first AE and is about to hire the first SDR (per mod-107 Chapter 3).

**Step 1 — Where does the ICP look?** Loomly's ICP is platform engineers and engineering managers at mid-market B2B SaaS companies. Jane maps their existing information graph:
- **Search:** they Google for "detect stale services in monorepo", "renovate config alternatives", "OpenTelemetry service graph", "how to enforce ownership on Terraform modules".
- **Peer discussion:** Hacker News, r/devops, Lobsters, LeadDev Slack, Rands Leadership Slack, the platform-engineering community on the CNCF Slack.
- **Newsletters:** Bytes.dev, TLDR, Kelsey Hightower's writing, Engineering Enablement Weekly.
- **Video / podcasts:** LeadDev's YouTube, Software Engineering Daily, Platform Engineering Podcast, DevTools FM.
- **Events:** KubeCon, PlatformCon, LeadDev, SRECon.
- **Not present in meaningful volume:** LinkedIn Ads (VP-Eng persona is on LinkedIn but does not respond to ads), IT trade shows, Meta / Twitter display, gated whitepaper syndication.

**Step 2 — Where has founder-led sales produced signal?** Reviewing the 22 closed deals: 8 came from Jane's Twitter/X presence and technical writing (Jane has 8K followers, mostly platform engineers, and blogged sporadically); 6 came from cold outbound Jane wrote herself against a target-account list built from CNCF membership; 4 came from a KubeCon talk Jane gave; 3 came from HN comments Jane made on adjacent posts; 1 came from a customer referral. **The founder-led signal is concentrated in inbound / content and founder-led outbound, with events as a distant third.**

**Step 3 — What does the CAC math support?** ACV = $12-24K; 12-month payback at ~78% gross margin implies fully-loaded CAC ceiling of roughly $9-18K per closed customer (Chapter 2 develops the math in detail). Both founder-led outbound and inbound / content sit inside that envelope at seed scale; paid at the top of the funnel is technically inside the envelope but unproven for this ICP. Partnerships and community are inside the envelope but slower to signal.

**Step 4 — Pick the primary.** Two candidates score highest: **founder-led outbound** (fastest time-to-signal; already proven; matches the SDR-AE motion Jane just hired for) and **inbound / content + SEO** (matches the ICP's information graph; produced 8 of the 22 founder-led deals; compounds over time). Jane picks **founder-led outbound** as the Q1 primary — its shorter time-to-signal matches the newly-hired AE's ramp timeline (mod-107 Chapter 3) and it is the direct predecessor of the SDR motion she is standing up. The AE and Jane split it: AE runs cadence execution against 200 target accounts; Jane hand-runs the top-30 tier with Level-3 personalisation (mod-107 Chapter 3's cadence discipline).

**Step 5 — Pick the experimental.** **Inbound / content + SEO** is the natural experimental — long time-to-signal, compounds, and hedges the outbound primary. Jane commits to *one* SEO-ranked deep-dive per month (four in the quarter) plus one HN-optimised technical post per month, funded at ~20% of minimum viable investment. Distribution is via Jane's Twitter, the two engineer-focused newsletters she has relationships with, and cross-posts on Hacker News. Attribution: UTM-tagged links, HubSpot's first-touch attribution report, weekly review with the AE to flag inbound-driven demo requests.

**Step 6 — Explicitly rule out.** Everything else goes on the "not now" list:
- **Paid acquisition** — not now. The ICP is not on the paid channels Loomly could plausibly buy; running $10K/month of Google Ads on "monorepo" and "platform engineering" keywords at seed scale would burn budget on click-throughs from developers with no buying authority. Re-evaluate in 12 months once ICP-fit is proven and there is inbound content on the site to which paid can drive traffic.
- **Community** — not now, with a caveat. Jane is *personally* active in the CNCF and LeadDev Slacks, and treats that as founder presence, not as a Loomly community motion. Building a dedicated Loomly community (a Slack or Discord for users) is a mod-109 retention motion (mod-109 Chapter 5), not a demand-gen channel at seed scale. Re-evaluate at 100 paying customers.
- **Partnerships / channel** — not now. One inbound conversation with a systems integrator is logged and dated; re-evaluate at Series A when the ICP list has been exhausted at the direct level.
- **Events / DevRel** — not now, with a caveat. Jane will accept one keynote invitation if it comes up organically (KubeCon Europe is likely) but is not proactively pursuing events as a channel. Re-evaluate at Series A.

**Step 7 — Instrument.** HubSpot dashboards track: outbound cadence touches / week, outbound reply rate, outbound demos booked, outbound closed-won ARR; inbound article publish rate, article views, article-to-signup conversion, signup-to-demo conversion, inbound closed-won ARR. Weekly review with the AE; monthly review of the experimental channel against the Chapter 2 channel-market-fit criteria. Explicit no-fly-zone: no ad-hoc "let's try a Discord this week" side quests; anything that isn't the primary or the experimental goes on the "not now" backlog with a date.

The portfolio decision, made this way, is one page of rationale plus the "not now" list. It is defensible against a board question ("why aren't you running paid?") because the reasoning traces to the ICP + the founder-led signal + the CAC math. It is diagnosable six months in ("outbound converted at 32% but reply rate is falling; inbound has three articles ranking top-10 and driving 400 signups/month — should we swap primary and experimental?") because both channels are instrumented against the same criteria. Six channels each funded at 15% of minimum viable investment would have produced none of that.

## Common failure patterns

- **Six-channel-parade trap.** Founder tries to run outbound + inbound + community + paid + partnerships + events simultaneously, each at 15-20% of minimum viable investment. All six produce diagnostic-invisible results. Founder concludes "our market is hard" when the actual answer is that no channel was funded to the level where it could have produced signal. Fix: one primary + one experimental; the rest go to "not now" with a date.
- **Channel-of-fashion trap.** Founder picks the channel currently discussed on Twitter / First Round Review / at the last conference — regardless of whether the ICP is reachable there. Community had a moment in 2020-2022; DevRel had a moment in 2021-2023; AI-generated content had a moment in 2023-2025. Fix: the channel decision routes through the ICP's information graph, not the founder's Twitter feed.
- **Career-history-channel trap.** Founder picks the channel her last company ran. Ex-HubSpot founders default to inbound; ex-Outreach founders default to outbound; ex-Notion founders default to community. Sometimes the ICP overlaps and the channel choice happens to be right; often it does not. Fix: derive from the ICP + the founder-led signal, not from career history.
- **Investor-said-so trap.** A board member says "you need to be doing paid" or "you need SEO". Founder installs the channel without doing the three-question test. The board member is often right about the *category* but wrong about the *stage*; a channel that will be the primary at Series B is not necessarily the right primary at seed. Fix: capture the board input as a hypothesis on the "not now" list with a date-triggered re-evaluation, rather than reflexively installing it.
- **Discount-the-working-channel trap.** Founder's own writing has produced 8 of 22 founder-led deals. Founder concludes "that was luck, we need a scalable channel" and picks paid instead. The founder just discounted the one channel with actual revenue signal in favour of a channel with none. Fix: the channel that produced founder-led revenue is the strongest working hypothesis for the primary; do not discount it just because it feels un-repeatable.
- **Experimental-as-decoration trap.** Founder commits to inbound content as the experimental channel but does not assign a writer, does not set a publishing cadence, does not instrument attribution. The "experimental" slot on the portfolio doc is empty. Fix: an experimental channel is a *real* commitment at exploratory scale; if there is no assignee, no calendar, and no instrumentation, it is a "not now" pretending to be an experiment.
- **No-not-now-list trap.** Founder rejects a channel this quarter but does not date the rejection or the re-evaluation criteria. Next quarter the same conversation re-opens from zero; the founder litigates the same channel decision every board meeting. Fix: the "not now" list is written, dated, and has an explicit re-evaluation trigger ("re-evaluate at 100 paying customers", "re-evaluate at Series A", "re-evaluate at ACV expansion into $100K enterprise band").
- **Time-to-signal ignorance trap.** Founder runs an inbound content channel for 8 weeks, concludes "SEO doesn't work for us", pivots to paid. Inbound's time-to-signal is 6-12 months; 8 weeks is diagnostic-invisible. The founder just killed a channel before its window opened. Fix: the time-to-signal window is set at portfolio-decision time and adhered to; the channel is either successful or explicitly failed *after* the window, not before.
- **No-motion-fit check trap.** Founder picks paid as the primary against a $12K-ACV SDR-AE motion; paid produces low-intent MQLs that the SDR-AE cadence cannot convert. The channel is producing volume but not leads the motion can close. Fix: the channel choice has to fit the mod-107 motion — a mismatch is diagnosable the same way as a motion-ICP mismatch (leads that don't convert at the same step).
- **Confuse-founder-presence-with-community-motion trap.** Founder is personally active in a Slack, participates in HN, blogs on her personal site. Founder concludes "community is our primary channel". This is founder presence, not a community motion (Chapter 5 develops the distinction). Fix: a community motion has an owned surface (the community exists because of the company), a defined membership shape, and a cadence; founder presence in *someone else's* community is a form of inbound / content, not community.
- **All-experiments-no-primary trap.** Founder is uncomfortable committing to one channel and instead runs "three experiments." Every channel is at exploratory scale; none is at primary scale; none can succeed. Fix: an experiment is defined against a primary; if there is no primary, there are no experiments — there is just a scattered set of half-funded channels.

## Summary

- Every channel has a **minimum viable investment level** below which it produces no signal; six channels each funded at 15-20% of minimum viable investment produce zero repeatable channels.
- The doctrine — **one primary channel funded to 100% of minimum viable investment, plus one experimental channel funded to 20-40% at exploratory scale — with everything else on a dated "not now" list.** Descends from Weinberg & Mares's Bullseye, Balfour's Four-Fit, and Thiel's *Zero to One* distribution chapter.
- The **primary** is picked against three questions: **where does the ICP already look for solutions**, **where has founder-led sales already produced signal**, and **what CAC does the ACV + payback math support?** The channel that scores best across the three is the primary.
- The **experimental** is a real commitment at exploratory scale with three jobs: **hedge against the primary failing**, **generate the next-stage hypothesis**, and **cover a strategic gap the primary does not**. It has an assignee, a calendar, an attribution setup — it is not a decoration.
- Every channel has a **time-to-signal** window — 4-8 weeks for outbound, 6-12 weeks for paid, 3-9 months for partnerships and events, 6-12 months for inbound, 6-18 months for community. The window is set at portfolio-decision time; the channel is not killed before its window opens.
- **Do not run two primaries** at seed-to-Series-A stage. The fixed cost of each channel's expertise and attention exceeds what a small team can pay across two. The only exception is a stacked motion where the two channels feed different stages of the funnel — and even then the founder should be able to name the routing boundary.
- The **six channel archetypes** the module develops: founder-led outbound (Chapter 4), inbound / content + SEO (Chapter 3), community (Chapter 5), paid acquisition (Chapter 6), plus partnerships / channel and events / DevRel (touched in Chapters 5 and 6 as adjacent). Every channel has a matched motion from mod-107.
- The **matched-motion check**: the channel choice has to be compatible with the mod-107 sales motion. A channel that produces leads the motion cannot convert is a channel-motion mismatch and fails the same way as a motion-ICP mismatch.
- Chapter 2 develops the **channel-market-fit diagnosis** — the CAC / payback math, ICP-fit lead quality, and the time-to-signal window that tells you whether the primary is actually working. Chapters 3-6 develop each archetype in depth. Chapter 7 develops the **channel-experimentation cadence** — how the portfolio evolves over quarters as channels decay (Andrew Chen's law of shitty click-throughs).
