# Resources — mod-105 Founder-Led Sales

Citable references the module is grounded in. Primary sources and canonical practitioner texts first; adjacent operational writing second. Every chapter cites at least one item on this list; every module-level claim about a named framework (Kazanjy's six-stage model, SPICED, MEDDIC / MEDDPICC, Winning by Design SPICED, Sales Acceleration Formula) traces back to a primary source below.

## Founding sales — the spine of the module

- **Peter Kazanjy — *Founding Sales: The Early-Stage Go-To-Market Handbook* (self-published, continuously updated).** The book this module operationalises. Kazanjy is the co-founder of Atrium (sales analytics) and the founder of Modern Sales Pros, the ~15K-member sales operators community. The book is the most-cited practitioner text on the founder-driven, customer-development-fused motion before the first sales hire; the six-stage model (prospect → outreach → discovery → demo → proposal → close) in Chapter 1 is Kazanjy's directly. Full book available free online: [https://www.foundingsales.com/](https://www.foundingsales.com/). Companion community: [Modern Sales Pros](https://www.modernsalespros.com/).
- **Peter Kazanjy — talks and essays.** Kazanjy's talk library at First Round, SaaStr, and Modern Sales Pros events substantially tracks the book but with updated operator anecdotes. ## Qualification methodologies — SPICED and MEDDIC / MEDDPICC

The discovery call framework choice by ACV band is Chapter 3's central routing. SPICED for mid-market and below; MEDDIC / MEDDPICC when ACV crosses the SMB threshold. Both methodologies have primary-source canons.

### SPICED — Winning by Design

- **Winning by Design — the SPICED methodology library.** SPICED (**S**ituation, **P**ain, **I**mpact, **C**ritical event, **D**ecision) was developed by Jacco van der Kooij's Winning by Design as a buyer-centered alternative to MEDDIC. Free content library including the SPICED cards and buyer-frame docs: [https://winningbydesign.com/resources/](https://winningbydesign.com/resources/).
- **Jacco van der Kooij and Fernando Pizarro — *Blueprints for a SaaS Sales Organization* (2015; 3rd ed. 2018).** The Winning by Design methodology in book form. Reference text for the consultative-selling buyer-frame treatment Chapter 3 cites. [https://www.amazon.com/Blueprints-SaaS-Sales-Organization-repeatable/dp/0996230122](https://www.amazon.com/Blueprints-SaaS-Sales-Organization-repeatable/dp/0996230122).
- **Winning by Design blog.** Operator-level posts on SPICED application, bowtie funnel, revenue architecture. [https://winningbydesign.com/blog/](https://winningbydesign.com/blog/).

### MEDDIC / MEDDPICC

- **MEDDIC origin.** Developed by Jack Napoli, Dick Dunkel, and John McMahon at PTC (Parametric Technology Corporation) in the 1990s during PTC's rapid enterprise-sales scale-up. The acronym: **M**etrics, **E**conomic Buyer, **D**ecision Criteria, **D**ecision Process, **I**dentify Pain, **C**hampion (MEDDIC); MEDDPICC extends with **P**aper Process and **C**ompetition. <!-- needs-research: pin a primary-source Jack Napoli / Dick Dunkel public account of MEDDIC's PTC origin — a published interview or MEDDICC.com official history page -->
- **John McMahon — *The Qualified Sales Leader: Proven Lessons from a Five Time CRO* (2021).** The most comprehensive practitioner treatment; McMahon co-developed MEDDIC at PTC. Required reading for the Chapter 3 "upgrade to MEDDIC above ACV threshold" argument. Amazon page: [https://www.amazon.com/Qualified-Sales-Leader-Proven-Lessons/dp/0578895064](https://www.amazon.com/Qualified-Sales-Leader-Proven-Lessons/dp/0578895064).
- **MEDDICC (organisation) — Andy Whyte — *MEDDICC: The Ultimate Guide to Staying One Step Ahead in the Complex Sale* (2020).** Practitioner reference book with the full MEDDPICC treatment, including the Paper Process and Competition extensions. Supplementary resources at [https://meddicc.com/](https://meddicc.com/).
- **Darius Lahoutifard — *Always Be Qualifying* (2019).** Alternative practitioner treatment of MEDDIC applied to enterprise B2B SaaS. Supplements McMahon as a second-opinion reference. [https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07WHKM73Z](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07WHKM73Z).

## Discovery and consultative selling — foundational

- **Neil Rackham — *SPIN Selling* (1988).** The research-anchored qualification methodology (**S**ituation / **P**roblem / **I**mplication / **N**eed-payoff) that is the methodological ancestor of both SPICED and MEDDIC. Chapter 3's "consultative discovery arc" descends directly from Rackham's research on complex-sale buyer behaviour. Fieldbook site: [https://www.spinsellingfieldbook.com/](https://www.spinsellingfieldbook.com/).
- **Mahan Khalsa and Randy Illig — *Let's Get Real or Let's Not Play: Transforming the Buyer / Seller Relationship* (2008).** Franklin Covey's complex-sales methodology on consultative discovery and buyer honesty — directly aligned with Chapter 3's "disqualification is a feature" framing. [https://www.franklincovey.com/the-7-habits/](https://www.franklincovey.com/the-7-habits/) (publisher page); [https://www.amazon.com/dp/1591842263](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1591842263).

[https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/311570/the-challenger-sale-by-matthew-dixon-and-brent-adamson/](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/311570/the-challenger-sale-by-matthew-dixon-and-brent-adamson/).

## Suggested reading order for this module

If starting cold:

1. Read [Peter Kazanjy — *Founding Sales*](https://www.foundingsales.com/) end-to-end (~4 hr). This is the module's spine; every subsequent read is a specific operational deepening.
2. Read [John McMahon — *The Qualified Sales Leader*](https://www.amazon.com/Qualified-Sales-Leader-Proven-Lessons/dp/0578895064) Parts 1-2 on MEDDIC + the buying-committee mapping (~2 hr).
3. Skim [Mark Roberge — *The Sales Acceleration Formula*](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Sales+Acceleration+Formula%3A+Using+Data%2C+Technology%2C+and+Inbound+Selling+to+go+from+%240+to+%24100+Million-p-9781119047070) Chapters 1-5 on ICP-driven repeatable motion and CRM instrumentation (~2 hr).
4. Read [Jacco van der Kooij and Fernando Pizarro — *Blueprints for a SaaS Sales Organization*](https://www.amazon.com/Blueprints-SaaS-Sales-Organization-repeatable/dp/0996230122) chapters on SPICED and the bowtie funnel (~2 hr).
5. Read [David Sacks — *The Cadence*](https://sacks.substack.com/) essay (~30 min) and skim [Kellblog](https://kellblog.com/) on pipeline-coverage math and forecast categories (~1 hr).
6. Read [Aaron Ross — *Predictable Revenue*](https://predictablerevenue.com/book) Chapters on Cold Calling 2.0 (~1.5 hr), scaled down to founder volume per Chapter 2.
7. Read [Andy Whyte — *MEDDICC*](https://meddicc.com/) if operating at MEDDPICC-band ACVs (~2 hr).
8. Skim [Trish Bertuzzi — *The Sales Development Playbook*](https://www.amazon.com/Sales-Development-Playbook-Build-Repeatable/dp/0692611657) for the outbound sequence discipline (~1.5 hr).

Total: ~16 hours of prerequisite reading before authoring a defensible founder-led-sales pack against the exercises.

## Provenance

This module cites primary sources first (Kazanjy's own writing on founding sales; McMahon's practitioner text as MEDDIC co-developer; van der Kooij's Winning by Design as SPICED's originator; Roberge's practitioner text on the data-driven sales motion; Sacks and Kellblog as the canonical operating-cadence practitioners). Framework references (SPICED, MEDDIC / MEDDPICC, SPIN, Challenger) trace to the practitioners who originated or codified each. Tool links are the actual tools referenced in the chapters' instrumentation discussions. Where a claim required a benchmark number (positive-reply rate on cold email, Gong Labs discovery-to-close correlation, Gartner buyer-committee size, Lemkin's "don't hire VP Sales before $1-2M ARR" cutoff), the citation is flagged `needs-research` until a specific primary source is pinned — the module ships with these flags visible rather than with fabricated benchmarks. If a factual claim in a chapter cannot be traced to one of the sources on this page (or to another primary source cited inline), it is a bug — please flag it via a `needs-research` comment against the offending chapter.
