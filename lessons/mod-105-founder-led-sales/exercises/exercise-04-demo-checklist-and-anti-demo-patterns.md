# Exercise 04 — Demo Checklist and Anti-Demo-Patterns Teardown

**Estimated time:** 3 hours
**Chapter link:** [`04-the-founder-demo-checklist-and-anti-patterns.md`](../04-the-founder-demo-checklist-and-anti-patterns.md)
**Prerequisite:** Chapter 4 read end-to-end; Exercise 02 (discovery script — the demo depends on the pain sized in discovery).

## Problem statement

Chapter 4 named the demo as the deal's second-most-consequential meeting: where the founder confirms the pain sized in discovery, shows the product addressing that pain concretely, surfaces objections, and either advances the deal to proposal or exits with disqualification. It also named the four anti-demo patterns — feature tour, demo-without-discovery, feature-promising, price-deflection — that convert a demo from deal-advancer to pipeline-debt-generator.

This exercise trains you to **author a working demo checklist** in the shipping shape from Chapter 4, **run (or role-play) 2 real demos** against it, and **teardown the demos** against the four anti-patterns with specific evidence per pattern. The output is a demo checklist you can hand to a first AE hire, plus a documented rehearsal that shows you catch the anti-patterns in your own delivery.

The failure mode this exercise exists to catch: **the founder authors a demo checklist that reads well as a document, runs demos ad-hoc anyway, and cannot cite specific evidence of when she used the checklist to catch herself feature-touring or feature-promising. The checklist is decoration; the anti-patterns still fire.**

## Requirements

Deliver a folder `exercise-04/` with:

- `part-a-demo-checklist.md` — the checklist artifact in the Chapter 4 shape.
- `part-b-demo-transcripts.md` — 2 demo transcripts (recorded live or role-played), with pre-demo confirmation records and post-demo notes.
- `part-c-anti-pattern-teardown.md` — the four-anti-pattern teardown per demo, with specific evidence.

### Part A — Author the demo checklist (45 min)

Deliver `part-a-demo-checklist.md`. Populate the Chapter 4 shipping-shape checklist with your specific product's content. Cover:

**Pre-demo (48 hours before) block:**
- Audience confirmation rule (buyer + champion + user, or reschedule).
- Agenda template — the specific 3-4 bullets you send.
- Pain-restate draft from discovery notes.
- Screenshots / data prep.

**Pre-demo (2 hours before) block:**
- Audience re-confirmation via email — the template.
- Tech check items (screen share, audio, notifications off).
- Pain recap read-aloud rehearsal.
- Backup plan for live-env failure — the specific static screenshots you have ready.

**Opening (2-3 min) block:**
- The framing sentence (this is about your pain, not our product).
- The pain-restate delivery — the exact language template with slots for the buyer's numbers.
- The arc-statement (2-4 workflows + ROI + next-step).

**Demo body (25-35 min) block:**
- Workflow 1: your product's specific workflow that addresses the most-common Pain-1 archetype from discovery.
- Workflow 2: the adjacent-value workflow — the second pain often surfaced with Pain-1.
- Workflow 3 (optional): the rollup / manager view — when to include, when to skip.
- ROI framing: the specific multiplier ranges you can cite (per Chapter 4's example) with the customer comparables — with permission and provenance.
- Pause + question language after every workflow.

**Close (5-10 min) block:**
- Objection surface language: "what would prevent your team from adopting this?"
- Handle-each-objection playbook: for the 5-8 most-common objections you expect, what is the specific response? (Address / commit / disqualify.)
- Next-step booking: language + calendar-pull mechanic.

**Post-demo (within 24 hours) block:**
- Next-step email template link (Exercise 05 will produce these).
- ROI one-pager format for champion enablement.
- CRM update fields (Chapter 7).
- Three-notes template (new language / surprise / objection pattern).

**Anti-pattern check block:**
- The four mental checks post-demo — did I feature-tour? skip discovery frame? feature-promise? deflect a price question?

Format as a single page (or two), versioned, dated, owned. The whole point is transferability — imagine handing it to a new hire on day one.

### Part B — Run 2 demos (90 min total)

Deliver `part-b-demo-transcripts.md`. Run **two** demos — recorded live if you have real deals, or role-played against peers/advisors playing buyers if not.

**Setup for each demo (5 min).**

- Real demo: the actual deal, actual buyer, actual champion, actual user.
- Role-play: brief the peer as in Exercise 02 — they play a buyer for one of your accounts, with a rough pain profile from a prior discovery role-play.

Both demos must include the pre-demo confirmations (audience, agenda, pain-restate) done in writing. Capture the confirmations as artifacts in Part B.

**Run each demo (30-40 min per demo).**

Use the checklist from Part A on-screen (or on a printed page next to you). Do not read verbatim — hold as scaffold.

Record if possible (audio + screen for real demos; audio at minimum for role-plays).

**Post-demo notes for each demo (10 min per demo).**

Immediately after each demo, write:

- The three notes from Chapter 3 (new language, surprise, next-step commitment).
- The specific next-step booked on the call (calendar invite sent live — screenshot or paste the invite).
- The objections raised and how each was handled (address / commit / disqualify).
- The one workflow that landed cleanest and the one that felt awkward.

Transcribe the salient exchanges (full transcription optional) — capture the moments needed for the Part C teardown.

### Part C — The four-anti-pattern teardown per demo (45 min)

Deliver `part-c-anti-pattern-teardown.md`. For each of the 2 demos, produce a teardown against all four anti-patterns:

```
Demo: {ID} — {account or role-play designation}

Anti-pattern 1 — Feature tour
  Did it fire? {yes / no / partially}
  Evidence: {specific moment — timestamp or transcript excerpt — where the demo drifted into feature-showing unrelated to discovery-surfaced pain, OR the specific moment you caught yourself and re-anchored}
  Corrective action: {what you would do differently next demo}

Anti-pattern 2 — Demo-without-discovery
  Did it fire? {yes / no}
  Evidence: {opening — did you restate discovery pain, or open with product overview?}
  Corrective action: {...}

Anti-pattern 3 — Feature-promising
  Did it fire? {yes / no / partially}
  Evidence: {any moment you answered "yes we do that" for a feature that does not exist, or committed to a ship date without contractual authority}
  Corrective action: {...}

Anti-pattern 4 — Price-deflection
  Did it fire? {yes / no}
  Evidence: {any price question — was it answered on the demo or deflected to "we'll cover at the end"}
  Corrective action: {...}

Bonus — Anti-pattern 5 (audience gap)
  Did the pre-demo audience confirmation hold? {yes / no}
  Evidence: {who was in the room; if buyer / champion / user was missing, name why the demo ran anyway}
```

Aggregate across the 2 demos: which anti-pattern fired most often across your delivery? That is the *specific* next iteration on the checklist.

## Starter guidance

- **The pre-demo confirmations are the leading indicator.** Every demo where the audience, agenda, or pain-restate was not confirmed in writing 48 hours ahead is a demo that is likely to fire multiple anti-patterns. Include the confirmation artifacts in Part B — screenshots of the emails, calendar invites, etc.
- **Workflows are tied to discovery pain, not to what you want to show.** The temptation to demo the feature you just shipped, or the workflow you are proudest of, is the specific origin of the feature-tour anti-pattern. Every workflow in Part A has to be explicitly tied to a discovery-surfaced pain archetype.
- **The pause-and-ask mechanic is what converts a monologue to a conversation.** Rehearse the specific pause language ("does this map to how your team handles this today?") — under time pressure, founders barrel through without pausing. Make the pause a scripted moment.
- **The objection-surface language is scripted.** "What would prevent your team from adopting this?" is the specific ask. Rehearse it; under pressure, founders skip it or soften it into "any questions?" — which produces polite silence, not real objections.
- **The ROI framing uses the buyer's number.** Not a generic industry statistic. If the buyer said $180K in delayed revenue during discovery, the ROI math starts from $180K. Founders who forget the buyer's number default to generic multipliers and lose the specificity.
- **Feature-promising is the most common founder anti-pattern.** Under real pressure from a buyer's "does it also do X?", the founder wants to say yes. Rehearse the honest alternative — "not yet — is that a hard requirement?" — until it is muscle memory.
- **Price-deflection is the second most common.** Rehearse the direct answer — "our current pricing for teams your size is $12K/year for the core tier; here's what's included" — so it lands cleanly when the buyer asks mid-demo.
- **The 2-demo minimum is deliberate.** One demo does not show pattern; two demos let you compare anti-pattern fires across delivery and identify which specific anti-pattern is *your* recurring weakness.
- **Role-play is legitimate if real demos are unavailable.** The anti-pattern discipline is what the exercise builds; the artificiality of role-play is fine as long as the peer is honestly playing a resistant buyer.

Your submission is complete when:

- [ ] Every workflow in Part A is explicitly tied to a discovery-surfaced pain archetype.

- [ ] Part B contains 2 demo transcripts (live or role-played), with pre-demo confirmation artifacts (audience, agenda, pain-restate) captured.
- [ ] Both demos include post-demo notes: three-notes writeup, next-step-booked evidence (calendar invite), objection handling, workflow-landing self-assessment.
- [ ] Part C contains a full four-anti-pattern teardown per demo (evidence + corrective action for each), plus the bonus audience-gap check.

## Common ways this exercise goes wrong

- **The workflow-not-tied-to-pain trap.** Workflows in Part A are showcase workflows, not pain-tied. Every demo becomes a feature tour. Fix: every workflow in Part A explicitly names the discovery-surfaced pain archetype it addresses.
- **The no-pre-demo-confirmations trap.** Founder ran the demos without the 48-hour audience / agenda / pain-restate. Buyer + user missing; demo lands weakly. Fix: confirmations are the demo's leading indicator; include the artifacts in Part B.
- **The one-demo-only trap.** Founder ran one demo and skipped the second. No pattern is visible. Fix: 2 demos minimum; the pattern is the point.
- **The self-flattering teardown trap.** All four anti-patterns "did not fire" in Part C. Fix: honest evidence per pattern — even a partial-fire is worth capturing. If you truly did not fire any of the four, the teardown should include the specific moment you caught yourself and re-anchored.
- **The generic-objection-playbook trap.** Objection playbook has "if buyer objects on price, offer discount" — not specific enough. Fix: 5-8 named objections with specific address / commit / disqualify language.
- **The generic-ROI-multiplier trap.** ROI framing uses "customers typically save 3x" without named customer comparables. Fix: real customers with permission, or `needs-research` flag.
- **The read-verbatim checklist trap.** Founder reads Part A on the demo like a script. Delivery robotic. Fix: hold as scaffold; deliver conversationally.
- **The no-post-demo-notes trap.** Founder skipped the three-notes writeup. Customer-development half of the demo lost. Fix: notes within 2 hours; template in Part A.
- **The feature-promise-caught-but-not-corrected trap.** Founder notes "I said yes to a feature we don't have" in Part C and does not name the corrective action. Fix: every fired pattern has a specific corrective action for the next demo.
- **The audience-gap-glossed-over trap.** Buyer or user was missing from the demo and Part C does not flag it. Deal will likely stall regardless of demo quality. Fix: audience-gap check is bonus but load-bearing; call it out honestly.
- **The no-next-step-on-demo trap.** Demo ended with "we'll send some info." Deal enters pipeline debt. Fix: calendar invite live on the demo, screenshot in Part B.
- **The three-improvements-not-shipped trap.** Part C produces good analysis; Part A checklist is not updated. Fix: v1.1 of the checklist shipped based on the teardown findings; the exercise's whole point is the iteration.
