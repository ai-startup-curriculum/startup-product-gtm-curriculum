# Exercise 05 — Next-Step Discipline Drill

**Estimated time:** 2 hours
**Chapter link:** [`05-next-step-discipline-on-every-call.md`](../05-next-step-discipline-on-every-call.md)
**Prerequisite:** Chapter 5 read end-to-end; Exercises 02 (discovery role-play) and 04 (demo transcripts) so real / role-played calls exist to audit against.

## Problem statement

Chapter 5 named the universal rule: every conversation ends with a scheduled next step booked on the calendar before the call ends, or an explicit disqualification with a reason coded. "Let me follow up next week" is not an option. Vague follow-ups bundle three failure modes — commitment slip, context decay, commitment asymmetry — and produce pipeline debt that looks fine for a month and is un-recoverable by month three.

This exercise trains you to **author the five next-step email templates** (one per stage transition), **audit your existing pipeline against the "% with calendared next-step" metric**, and **rehearse the four buyer-resistance responses** (let-me-check-my-team, send-me-info, silent-buyer, defer-to-Q3) with pre-loaded language. For MEDDPICC-range deals, author a **mutual action plan (MAP)** template. The output is a working next-step pack that installs the discipline into the founder's real weekly cadence, not just documents it.

The failure mode this exercise exists to catch: **the founder reads Chapter 5, nods, agrees the discipline is right, and continues to end real calls with "let me follow up." The chapter's discipline is understood intellectually but not installed operationally. Pipeline debt continues to accumulate.**

## Requirements

Deliver a folder `exercise-05/` with:

- `part-a-email-templates.md` — the five next-step email templates + the MAP template.
- `part-b-pipeline-audit.md` — the % calendared next-step audit of the current pipeline (real or working sample) with per-deal resolutions.
- `part-c-resistance-scripts.md` — the four buyer-resistance response scripts, rehearsed.

### Part A — Author the five next-step email templates + the MAP (45 min)

Deliver `part-a-email-templates.md`. Author each template with clearly-marked personalisation slots, a next-step ask, and confirmation-of-what-was-agreed.

**Template 1 — Post-discovery → demo-booked.**

Structure per Chapter 5:
- Confirms the demo audience, agenda, and pain framing (in the buyer's language).
- Names the deliverable (agenda attached / linked).
- Cross-references the calendar invite sent live on the call.
- Single ask: "please forward to {names} if any are missing from the invite."

**Template 2 — Post-demo → proposal-in-flight.**

- Confirms the proposal delivery date.
- Confirms the pricing framing shared verbally on the demo.
- Restates the ROI in the buyer's language.
- Cross-references the calendar invite for the proposal-review call.
- Single ask: "please forward the ROI one-pager to {economic buyer / champion's boss}."

**Template 3 — Post-proposal → close-plan-review.**

- Confirms the redline path (owner + target date for round 1).
- Confirms the countersigner names.
- Confirms the close date.
- Links the mutual-action-plan (Template 6 below) for MEDDPICC deals.
- Single ask: "please introduce me to {procurement / security lead / economic buyer} this week for {specific step}."

**Template 4 — Post-close → kickoff-booked.**

- Confirms the kickoff date and CSM handoff (or founder-as-CSM stopgap).
- Confirms the success-metric baseline (from Chapter 6's proposal Implementation section).
- Cross-references the kickoff calendar invite.
- Single ask: "please confirm {kickoff attendees} on the invite before Friday."

**Template 5 — Disqualification-clean-close.**

- Names the reason (specific — GitLab not GitHub, timing mismatch, ACV band).
- Refers to a partner or alternative where applicable.
- Commits to a dated reactivation if the unlock condition is real (with CRM tag confirmation).
- No fake warmth; clean and honest.

**Template 6 — Mutual action plan (MAP) template.**

For MEDDPICC-range deals, author a table shape following Chapter 5's example:

```
{Vendor} × {Customer} — Mutual Action Plan
Vendor lead: {name}  |  Customer lead: {name}

Step                                   Owner       Target date   Status
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Discovery + demo complete           joint       {date}        {status}
2. Proposal delivered                  vendor      {date}        {status}
3. Redline round 1                     customer    {date}        {status}
...
```

Include the standard step list (7-10 steps depending on ACV / procurement complexity), the update-cadence (weekly), and the field-owner rules (who updates what status).

### Part B — The pipeline audit (45 min)

Deliver `part-b-pipeline-audit.md`. Audit your current pipeline against Chapter 5's health metric — **% of open deals with a calendared next step in the next 14 days**.

If you have a real pipeline, use it. If you do not, construct a working sample of 8-12 deals across the six-stage model (real deals from Exercise 03 + fabricated / role-played deals from Exercises 02 and 04). Each deal has: name, stage, dollar value, current next step (if any), current next-step date (if any), the last touch date.

**B1 — The audit table.** Produce a table:

| Deal | Stage | ACV | Next-step (specific?) | Next-step date | Days since last touch | In next 14 days? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | Y/N |

Compute the **% with calendared next step in next 14 days**. Chapter 5 target: 85-100% healthy; 50-70% drifting; below 50% dead.

**B2 — The no-next-step column resolution.** For every deal in the "no calendared next-step" column, resolve it now (during the exercise). Chapter 5's rule: either book a specific new next-step on the calendar before the audit ends, or close-lost with a reason. Document each resolution:

```
Deal: {name}
Original state: no calendared next-step / next-step X days past due
Resolution: {booked new step on {date} with {ask}} OR {close-lost with reason code {code} — filed in CRM}
Language sent: {the exact language of the email or the CRM close-lost note}
```

Do this for real. The exercise is not to document what you *would* do; it is to actually resolve every no-next-step deal now.

**B3 — Pattern diagnosis (10 min).** Answer:

- What % of deals had no calendared next-step? Which stages were the offenders (Discovery, Demo, Proposal, Close)?
- How many deals close-losted during the resolution? What reason codes clustered?
- If the audit produced > 30% no-next-step, what specific discipline broke down (2-hour rule not applied post-call? weekly review not happening? next-step-live-on-call mechanic not rehearsed)?

### Part C — The four resistance-response scripts (30 min)

Deliver `part-c-resistance-scripts.md`. For each of Chapter 5's four resistance patterns, author the pre-loaded language you will use in real calls.

**Response 1 — "Let me get back to you with times."**

Author the specific language. Chapter 5's pattern: propose a placeholder-with-24-hour-firmup. Format:

```
Buyer says: "Let me get back to you with times."
You say: {your exact language, 2-3 sentences}
Fallback if they still resist: {what you say next}
```

**Response 2 — "I need to check with my team first."**

Author the specific language. Chapter 5's pattern: honest — "understood. What would you need from me to help you make the case?" plus a scaffolded next step (written summary, or a group session).

**Response 3 — "Send me some info and I'll look at it."**

Author the specific language. Chapter 5's pattern: gentle probing — "before I do, is this something you're likely to move on this quarter, or is it more of a research phase?"

**Response 4 — Silent buyer (no response after demo or proposal).**

Author the specific language for a 2-week 3-touch cadence ending in a break-up. Format:

```
Touch 1 (day 3 post-call): {your language}
Touch 2 (day 7 post-call): {your language}
Touch 3 — break-up (day 14 post-call): {your language, specifically}
```

The break-up is decision-forcing: "assuming this isn't the right time on your end — I'll close out my notes and reach back if things change on ours."

**Rehearsal check (5 min).** For each of the four, rehearse the language out loud twice. Note in the deliverable which of the four felt the most awkward — that is your specific practice target.

## Starter guidance

- **The five templates are working documents, not marketing copy.** Every sentence has a job — confirm-what-was-agreed, restate-pain-in-buyer-language, name-deliverable, cross-reference-calendar, single-ask. Cut every decorative sentence.
- **The single-ask discipline matters.** Buyers respond better to one specific ask than to three optional ones. If your Post-Discovery email asks the buyer to (a) forward to the CFO, (b) review a case study, and (c) confirm attendance — you get zero of the three. Pick one; get the one.
- **The 2-hour rule is what makes the emails work.** A template sent 24 hours later has to re-establish context; a template sent within 2 hours inherits the buyer's mental state for free. Build the reflex — "close the call, open the template, send it."
- **The MAP is a shared artifact, not an internal doc.** Share it with the champion (and buyer where appropriate); update it weekly; use it in the weekly pipeline review. A MAP the vendor maintains privately is not a MAP.
- **The pipeline audit has to be real.** If you skip Part B by writing "I will do this next week," the exercise's discipline-installation half is skipped. Audit today, resolve today.
- **The disqualification-close-lost is the second permitted outcome — use it.** If Part B's audit produces zero close-losts, either your pipeline is unnaturally clean or you are avoiding the disqualification. Chapter 5's target: 30-50% qualified-out rate is healthy.
- **The resistance responses are memorised, not read.** Under real pressure, having pre-loaded language is what enables the discipline. Rehearse the 4 out loud twice; the one you cannot deliver smoothly is the one that costs deals.
- **The break-up email is not aggressive.** It is graceful decision-forcing. "Assuming this isn't the right time" is the tone; not "let me know or I'll close you out." Rehearse the graceful tone.
- **The pipeline-audit % is the leading indicator, not the outcome.** Every week the founder reports the number — 85-100% healthy, downward trend is a warning. Include the number in Chapter 7's weekly review.

## Common ways this exercise goes wrong

- **The template-written-but-not-used trap.** Templates in Part A read well but the founder does not send them within 2 hours of real calls. Pattern in Part B shows the discipline break. Fix: the templates are for use, not display.
- **The pipeline-audit-fabricated trap.** Founder scripts an audit that shows 90% compliance because they filled in placeholder next-steps. Fix: audit the real state, not the aspirational one; the honest number is the useful signal.
- **The no-close-losts trap.** Every deal in the no-next-step column gets a rescued next-step; none are close-losted. Fix: some fraction has to close-lost — that is the disqualification discipline at work.
- **The single-ask-violated trap.** Templates ask 3-4 things per email. Buyers respond to none. Fix: one specific ask per email; move the others to subsequent emails.
- **The MAP-authored-not-shared trap.** MAP is a beautiful internal doc the champion never sees. Fix: MAP is shared with champion (and buyer); updated weekly; part of the deal artifact set.
- **The resistance-scripts-hand-waved trap.** Part C has one sentence per response. Fix: full language, plus a fallback, plus a rehearsal check.
- **The break-up-too-aggressive trap.** Response 4's break-up reads as "reply or I'll disqualify." Fix: graceful "assuming this isn't the right time" tone.
- **The 2-hour-rule-ignored trap.** Templates sent 24-48 hours post-call. Attendance rate on subsequent meetings drops. Fix: the 2-hour rule is the mechanic; build the reflex.
- **The nurture-inflates-pipeline trap.** Deals moved to Nurture but still counted in active pipeline. Forecast breaks. Fix: Nurture is a separate CRM state; not in active pipeline.
- **The stakeholder-intro-drift trap.** Champion promised an intro; three weeks later no intro. Founder does not push. Fix: soft nudge at week 1, forwardable email offer at week 2, honest "is this still real?" at week 3.
- **The no-pattern-diagnosis trap.** Part B is audited but the pattern is not diagnosed. Fix: name the stages that clustered, name the discipline that broke down, name the specific fix.
- **The rehearsal-in-your-head trap.** Founder claims to have rehearsed the resistance scripts silently. Fix: out loud, twice; note which one felt awkward.
- **The one-time-not-recurring trap.** The audit happens once; the % metric is never revisited. Fix: the metric is a weekly review item in Chapter 7; the pack ships with the weekly cadence installed.
