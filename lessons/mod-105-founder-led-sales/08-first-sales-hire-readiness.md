# First Sales Hire Readiness — Diagnosing the Hand-off

## Motivation

The founder-led sales motion has an intended lifespan: it exists to close the first 20-30 customers by hand and to produce the pack of artifacts a first sales hire can inherit. It is not a permanent operating mode. Founders who cling to it — running discovery, demo, and close personally on every deal at year 3 — throttle the business at their own calendar. Founders who exit it too early — hiring an AE or VP Sales in month 4 before the motion has been characterised — burn the hire's ramp on a script that does not exist.

The hand-off timing is where founder-led sales most-often breaks. Two symmetric failure modes:

- **The premature-hire trap.** Founder is uncomfortable with sales, closes a handful of deals under duress, hires a "sales leader" in month 4 to take over. The hire arrives to no ICP scorecard, no discovery script, no demo checklist, no rate card, no CRM discipline. She spends 6 months trying to invent what the founder should have characterised, produces uneven results, is fired at month 10, the founder concludes "sales hiring is broken," and the motion re-sets to zero.
- **The prolonged-founder-led trap.** Founder closes deals 30 through 60 personally, gets addicted to the customer contact, refuses to hire, plateaus at ~$3-5M ARR against her calendar constraint. The company is real but stuck; the co-founder or the seed board eventually forces the hire, at which point the motion is over-characterised to the founder's personal quirks and no one else can run it cleanly.

Between the two lies a narrower window than most founders realise — the specific range where the motion is *repeatable enough to hand off* and *painful enough that the founder is genuinely the bottleneck*. This chapter defines the diagnostic that identifies the window, the ramp plan for the first hire, and the founder-out plan that specifies what the founder still does after the hire (and what she deliberately stops doing).

The failure mode this chapter exists to catch: **the founder hires the first sales person based on urgency ("I don't have time") or investor pressure ("you need a VP Sales") instead of on the diagnostic signals that say the pack is ready — and either the wrong hire is made, or the right hire is made too early to succeed.**

The remedy: a **five-signal readiness diagnostic**, a **first-hire role decision** (SDR vs. first AE vs. Head of Sales, matched to the specific evidence in the pack), a **60-90 day ramp plan** that hands the pack piece-by-piece, and a **founder-out plan** that names what the founder still owns (strategic accounts, above-threshold ACV, product-tied deals) and what she stops owning (everything else). The hand-off is a *characterised transition*, not an event.

## Core concepts

### The five readiness signals

The motion is ready to hand off when *all five* of the following signals fire. Individually, any one signal can look positive; collectively, they are the diagnostic. Missing any signal is a specific piece of the pack that is not yet ready — the founder's next investment is that missing piece, not the hire.

**Signal 1 — Repeatable discovery script.**

The founder is running roughly the same discovery script call to call. New questions are added rarely; the script has been stable for 4-6 weeks. The 8-12-question SPICED / MEDDIC arc from Chapter 3 has been executed on at least 15-20 real calls, and the founder can hand another person the written script and know the coverage will be complete.

**Signal 2 — Repeatable win pattern.**

Looking at the last 10-15 closed-won deals, the founder can name the shared shape:

- The dominant reason code (Chapter 7's `PAIN_URGENT` / `ROI_CLEAR` / `CHAMPION_STRONG` / etc. — one or two codes cover 60-80% of wins).
- The common ICP profile (mod-104 Layer 1 + Layer 2 criteria) that most winners fit.
- The common critical-event pattern that forced the decision.
- The common ACV band ± 30-50%.
- The common cycle length ± 30-50%.

If the winning deals look like a scattered cloud with no visible pattern, the motion has produced revenue but not characterisation. The first hire will not be able to reproduce the pattern because the pattern does not yet exist.

**Signal 3 — Repeatable loss pattern (the disqualifier signal).**

Looking at the last 10-15 closed-lost deals, the founder can name the dominant loss reasons:

- Most losses cluster in 2-3 reason codes (typically `NOT_ICP`, `NO_URGENCY`, `FEATURE_GAP`).
- The disqualifier checklist (mod-104 Chapter 3) has been *applied* on losses — the losses look like the ICP scorecard failing correctly, not like the ICP scorecard being ignored.
- The losses feed a legible ICP-drift diagnosis (mod-104 Chapter 7) — every quarter's losses either confirm the current scorecard or produce a specific v-next scorecard edit.

If loss reasons are scattered across every code or a majority land in a `MISC` bucket, the disqualification discipline is not yet strong enough to hand to an AE who does not have the founder's tacit knowledge.

**Signal 4 — Founder as bottleneck (the demand-side signal).**

The pack is characterised but the founder cannot personally serve the demand. Specific signals:

- Inbound demo requests take > 5 business days to schedule because the founder's calendar is full.
- The founder is turning away qualified accounts because she cannot get to them within the buyer's timeframe.
- Cycle time is stretching (deal-to-deal comparison) because the founder cannot get to critical next-steps within the buyer's expected latency.
- The founder is spending > 40% of her working hours on sales (call this the "founder-time-on-sales" number) and other founder-critical work (product, fundraising, team) is being deprioritised.

The founder-as-bottleneck signal without the previous three signals is a *premature-hire trap* — the founder is overwhelmed but the pack is not ready. Hiring will not help until the pack is characterised.

**Signal 5 — The pack ships to a colleague and produces recognisable behaviour.**

Concrete test: the founder hands the complete pack (ICP scorecard, cold outreach sequence, discovery script, demo checklist, proposal template, next-step email templates, rate card, CRM stage definitions) to a trusted colleague — a co-founder, a senior IC, an advisor with sales experience — and asks them to run a mock discovery call and a mock demo against a role-played prospect. If the colleague produces a call that a real prospect would recognise as competent (right shape, right questions, right disqualifier hooks), the pack is ready. If the colleague reads the pack, runs the call, and produces something the founder would not send a real prospect into, the pack has gaps.

This is a *substitution* test — the pack has to work when the founder is not in the room. Founders under-run this test because it feels artificial; running it (even once, informally) is one of the highest-leverage 60 minutes in the whole module.

### The four negative signals — false readiness

Founders sometimes read *the wrong signals* as readiness. Four false positives worth naming:

- **"We hit our number this quarter."** Revenue outcomes are lagging indicators of many things — a lucky critical-event on one big deal, a champion-driven referral wave, a competitor's failure — and do not confirm the pack is characterised. A quarter of good revenue with no discernible pattern is not a signal.
- **"An investor / board member said we need a VP Sales."** External pressure is a real force but not a diagnostic. Hire based on the five signals; if an investor is pushing on timing, walk them through the diagnostic and either agree the signals fire or explain what specifically is still missing.
- **"A great candidate is on the market and I might miss my window."** Availability of a specific candidate is not a signal that the motion is ready. If the pack is not ready, the great candidate will fail; hiring against candidate-availability produces the premature-hire trap.
- **"The founder is tired of selling."** Founder discomfort with sales is real and is a legitimate personal preference, but it is not a signal that the pack is ready. If the founder wants to exit sales because it is not her calling, the answer is still to characterise the pack first (perhaps with a sales advisor's help) and hire second. The alternative — hiring to escape — trades a personal discomfort for a strategic failure.

The five real signals fire on evidence in the pack, the CRM, and the win/loss pattern. The false signals fire on emotion, external pressure, or lagging outcomes. The discipline is running the five-signal check honestly.

### The first-hire role decision — SDR, first AE, or Head of Sales

Once the diagnostic fires, the founder still faces a role choice — the wrong role is a wasted hire even when the motion is ready. Three shapes to consider.

**Option A — SDR (Sales Development Representative).**

The SDR runs top-of-funnel — Chapter 2's outbound sequence at higher volume than the founder can personally sustain. Discovery, demo, proposal, and close remain with the founder. The founder is still in every deal past the first meeting.

- **When it fits.** The founder is bottlenecked on outbound volume; discovery-through-close is still fun and manageable at her hours; the outbound sequence is validated (Chapter 2's reply-rate and positive-reply-rate math is stable); the founder wants 6-12 more months of personal deal contact before delegating downstream.
- **When it fails.** The pack downstream of Discovery is not characterised — SDR books meetings but founder cannot process them at pace. Front-of-funnel is fine; back-half is the actual bottleneck.
- **Cost.** Lower salary + commission than a first AE. Typically 6-10 months to a productive-outbound baseline.

**Option B — First AE (Account Executive).**

The first AE runs the full motion — discovery through close — for a defined slice of the pipeline. Founder still owns strategic accounts and above-threshold ACV; AE owns the operating range.

- **When it fits.** The pack is complete and has passed the Signal-5 substitution test. Cycle length and win rate are stable enough to write a quota. Founder is genuinely bottlenecked across the whole motion, not just outreach.
- **When it fails.** The pack is not yet complete — AE ends up re-inventing discovery script or demo checklist. This is the classic premature-hire trap.
- **Cost.** Full AE package (base + variable, typically $180-260K OTE at SEED for a strong AE hire in B2B SaaS). Ramp typically 60-90 days to first close.

**Option C — Head of Sales (or VP Sales).**

Skips the individual-contributor first-AE step and hires a leader who will hire the team.

- **When it fits.** The motion is repeatable *and* the founder has a clear vision of a multi-person sales org within 6-12 months. Typically post-Series-A when the fundraise justifies the salary and the growth plan justifies the org shape.
- **When it fails.** Hired at SEED without the pack characterised — VP Sales spends first 6 months trying to reconstruct what the founder should have characterised. Empirically the most common expensive sales-hire failure. Multiple practitioner writers (Sacks, Roberge, Lemkin) explicitly warn against this pattern at SEED.
- **Cost.** VP-level compensation (typically $250-400K+ OTE at SEED / early Series A). Ramp typically 6-12 months to team-hire-and-productive.

**The default at SEED, post-diagnostic:** first AE. SDR is a good move when outbound is the specific bottleneck and downstream is fine. Head of Sales is rarely the right first hire at SEED; the diagnostic almost never justifies it before Series A.

### The 60-90 day AE ramp plan

Assuming a first-AE hire, the ramp plan is roughly a three-phase 60-90 day arc.

**Phase 1 — Absorb the pack (days 1-30).**

The AE reads the pack and shadows the founder. Specific milestones:

- **Days 1-7.** Read every artifact: ICP scorecard (mod-104), cold outreach sequence (Chapter 2), discovery script (Chapter 3), demo checklist (Chapter 4), proposal template + rate card (Chapter 6), next-step email templates (Chapter 5), CRM stage definitions (Chapter 7). Read the last 20 closed-won and 20 closed-lost deals in the CRM including all the reason codes.
- **Days 8-14.** Shadow the founder on 5-10 discovery calls and 3-5 demos. AE takes notes; no active role. Post-call debrief with founder — what would the AE have asked, what did the founder do that the AE would not have done, what surprised the AE?
- **Days 15-21.** AE writes her *own* version of the discovery script and demo checklist, based on the pack + what she absorbed shadowing. Founder reviews. Divergences are the raw material for improvement — either the founder learns something from the AE's version, or the AE learns why the founder's version is what it is. Both are healthy.
- **Days 22-30.** AE co-runs 3-5 discovery calls — she opens, the founder observes and jumps in only on stalls. Same for 1-2 demos. AE starts to own the CRM update discipline for those deals.

By end of Phase 1, the AE has *internalised* the pack, not just read it, and has run enough live conversations to identify the parts of the pack she has questions about.

**Phase 2 — Run supervised deals (days 31-60).**

The AE runs full deals — discovery through close — on a specific slice of the pipeline (typically the mid-market range, ACV $10-30K, cycle 4-8 weeks). Founder is on every call for the first 2-3 weeks, then only on demos and proposal reviews for the last 2. Specific milestones:

- **Weekly 1:1 (60 min).** AE walks every open deal with the founder. This is the same weekly review from Chapter 7, run against the AE's pipeline. Founder coaches on discipline, not on the individual deal.
- **First disqualification.** The AE has to explicitly close-lose a deal on the disqualifier checklist, with a reason code. This is a rite of passage — an AE who cannot say no in her first 30 days will never say no in her first year.
- **First proposal.** AE writes the proposal from the template. Founder reviews before send; typically the first 2-3 proposals need founder edits, after that the AE runs them herself.
- **First close.** Target is a first Closed-Won by day 60. If cycle length is longer, target is a first Closed-Won *cohort* — deals in Proposal or Close stage by day 60 that will land in month 3.

By end of Phase 2, the AE has closed a deal (or is about to) and is running the pack essentially independently on her slice of the pipeline.

**Phase 3 — Run unsupervised, iterate the pack (days 61-90).**

The AE runs her pipeline without founder on every call. Founder is on:

- Strategic-account calls (above-threshold ACV or logo-critical).
- Any deal where the AE explicitly requests founder involvement.
- The weekly 1:1 pipeline review.
- Any proposal or contract negotiation the AE flags.

The AE, by end of Phase 3, is ideally producing pack iterations herself — new questions added to the discovery script, new anti-pattern observed in a demo, new reason-code proposed for a loss the current list doesn't cover cleanly. These become the v-next pack.

If the AE is not iterating the pack by end of Phase 3, either the pack is already saturated at founder-level (unlikely) or the AE is a passive executor (concerning — flag for the 90-day review).

### The founder-out plan — what the founder still owns

Handing the motion off is not "founder never sells again." It is a redistribution. The founder still owns specific work post-hire; being explicit about what she still owns is what prevents both under-delegation (founder still in every deal) and over-delegation (founder disappears entirely and loses ICP / product feedback).

A working founder-out split:

**Founder still owns (post-hire):**

- **Strategic accounts.** A specific list of 5-15 named accounts — logo-critical, industry-shaping, or reference-worthy — that the founder personally runs regardless of ACV. The list is written; the AE knows which accounts are off-limits.
- **Above-threshold ACV.** Deals above a defined ACV band (e.g., > $75K at SEED) get the founder involved in demo, proposal, and close. The AE runs discovery and coordinates; the founder is a specific participant in the critical meetings.
- **Product-tied deals.** Deals whose scope requires a product commitment (feature date, custom integration, unusual deployment) get the founder at the proposal stage — she is the only person with the authority to make the commitment.
- **Weekly pack iteration.** Founder still owns the pack itself. The AE proposes edits; the founder approves and ships the v-next. This is the primary connective tissue that keeps the pack from drifting from what actually works.
- **Weekly pipeline review with the AE.** 60 minutes per week, same agenda as Chapter 7. Founder is coach, not player.
- **Monthly ICP / pricing / product feedback rollup.** The reason-code rollup from Chapter 7 feeds mod-104 (ICP drift), mod-106 (pricing), and the roadmap. Founder owns synthesis; AE contributes deal-level detail.

**Founder deliberately stops:**

- Discovery, demo, proposal, and close on the AE's assigned pipeline slice. If the AE needs help, she asks — the founder does not push in unsolicited.
- CRM data entry for the AE's deals. The AE owns her CRM state; the founder reads it, does not maintain it.
- Cold outreach on the AE's assigned segment. Outbound is the AE's or an SDR's; the founder does warm intros for strategic accounts only.
- Answering routine buyer questions the AE can answer (integration questions, pricing tier questions, timeline questions). The AE is empowered to answer; the founder resists the "let me jump in on this thread" reflex.

The **rule of thumb**: two months into the hire, the founder's time-on-sales should have dropped from 40%+ to 15-20% — reallocated to product, hiring, fundraising, or strategic-account depth. If the number has not dropped by month 2, the founder is under-delegating; if it has dropped to < 5%, the founder is over-delegating and the ICP / product feedback loop is at risk.

### The 90-day review — did the hand-off work?

At day 90, the founder runs a specific hand-off review. Three questions:

1. **Is the AE producing recognisable pack-shaped behaviour?** Discovery calls follow the script, demos follow the checklist, next-step discipline is enforced, CRM is clean, reason codes stamped. If any of these are missing, the ramp did not stick and the coaching investment continues (or the hire is not the right fit).
2. **Are pack iterations happening?** The AE is adding questions to the discovery script, refining the ROI framing, catching new anti-patterns. If she is passively executing the founder's pack unchanged, either the pack is perfect (unlikely at SEED) or the AE is not thinking critically about the motion (concerning).
3. **Is the founder-out reallocation real?** Founder's time-on-sales has dropped to 15-20%; the reclaimed hours are going somewhere identifiable (product / fundraising / hiring / strategic accounts) not into founder-anxiety pipeline-checking. If the reallocation has not happened, the founder is functionally still doing the sales job in parallel with the AE — the hire has not paid off.

A ramp that fails one of the three is diagnosable — pack gap (fix the pack), skill gap (more coaching or a different hire), or founder discipline gap (the founder has to actually let go). A ramp that fails two or more is a hard signal that either the hire, the timing, or the pack is wrong; the founder walks through mod-110's structured post-mortem before doubling down.

### The hand-off's downstream effects on the pack

The first AE hire changes the pack in three ways the founder should expect and prepare for.

- **The pack becomes multi-voice.** The founder wrote the pack in her own language and framing; the AE will re-language pieces of it to fit her own delivery. Some of the AE's re-language is superior; some is loss. The founder's job is to review v-next iterations for both, not to reject anything that changes her wording.
- **The CRM becomes multi-user.** Two people writing to the same CRM produces new hygiene questions — deal-ownership rules, notification patterns, review cadences. Chapter 7's five-field schema still holds; the ownership field becomes load-bearing.
- **Win/loss reasons diversify.** The AE will code losses the founder would have coded differently, and vice versa. The monthly review has to reconcile the coding conventions or the pattern signal degrades. This is a *governance* task the founder owns.

The pack that emerges after 90 days of AE work is *stronger* than the founder's solo pack — it has been tested against a second operator and iterated. This is the value of the hire that goes beyond raw revenue: the pack itself gets more robust.

### The bridge to mod-110

This chapter closes the mod-105 arc. The specific hand-off to mod-110 (GTM Metrics and First Hires):

- **The pack is the input** to mod-110's hiring plan. mod-110 develops the compensation design, the hiring rubric, the interview loop, the ramp instrumentation, and the multi-hire org shape (SDR + AE, or AE + AE, or first-manager) as the motion scales.
- **The five signals are the trigger** for mod-110's SEED-to-Series-A transition. When the signals fire and the first AE ramps successfully, the company is ready for the mod-110 metrics rigor (sales velocity equation, magic number, quota-attainment tracking).
- **The founder-out plan is the input** to mod-110's founder role redesign. Post-hire, the founder's job description changes; mod-110 develops the new job.

Founders skipping mod-105 and jumping directly to mod-110's hiring rubric produce hires who arrive to no pack. Founders running mod-105 without mod-110 produce a first hire who then plateaus at one AE with no path to a real sales org. Both modules are load-bearing; the transition point is Chapter 8's diagnostic.

## Concrete example — Loomly's month-12 diagnostic

Twelve months after starting founder-led sales, Loomly has 28 paying customers and Jane runs the readiness diagnostic.

**Signal 1 — Repeatable discovery script.** ✓ The 10-question SPICED script has been stable since month 8. Jane added two questions in month 6 (both around on-call state — Priya's question from Chapter 3), one question in month 9 (a critical-event probe on Q4 planning cycles), zero in months 10-12. Stable.

**Signal 2 — Repeatable win pattern.** ✓ Of 22 closed-won deals, 15 fit a common shape: 50-150 engineer B2B SaaS, GitHub, an urgent Q4 or Q1 planning goal on cycle time (SPICED "C" fires clean), $9-14K ACV, 6-8 week cycle, strong champion at EM level. Reason codes cluster in `PAIN_URGENT` (12) and `ROI_CLEAR` (8). The other 7 wins are outliers Jane can name individually.

**Signal 3 — Repeatable loss pattern.** ✓ Of 18 closed-lost deals, 12 fit two dominant reason codes: `NOT_ICP` — usually GitLab shops the outbound research missed (7), and `NO_URGENCY` — pain confirmed but no forcing event (5). The other 6 are scattered (`FEATURE_GAP` for SSO twice, `PROCESS_STALL` in enterprise, `LOST_TO_STATUS_QUO` twice). The mod-104 disqualifier list has been amended twice in the last quarter based on the `NOT_ICP` cluster — the drift loop is working.

**Signal 4 — Founder as bottleneck.** ✓ Jane's calendar shows 46% of hours on sales for the last 8 weeks — up from 32% six months ago. Inbound demo requests are averaging 6 business days to schedule; two accounts in the last month deferred to a competitor because Jane could not get on their calendar within their timeline. Product roadmap is falling behind because Jane cannot get 4-hour blocks for deep review sessions. The bottleneck is real and measurable.

**Signal 5 — Pack ships and produces recognisable behaviour.** Jane runs the substitution test — she asks the technical co-founder to run a role-played discovery call using the pack, against Jane as a role-played buyer. The co-founder produces a call that covers SPICED cleanly, catches the disqualifier when Jane role-plays a GitLab shop, and books a next step. Not smooth; recognisable. Pack works.

All five signals fire. Jane proceeds to the role decision.

**Role decision.** Jane considers all three options:

- **SDR?** Outbound is producing 4-5 first meetings per week — not the top bottleneck. Discovery, demo, and close are where Jane's calendar is compressed. SDR would solve the wrong problem.
- **Head of Sales?** Too early. Loomly is at $340K ARR run-rate; one AE will double capacity, not require a multi-person org. Head of Sales at SEED against 1 IC is over-hired.
- **First AE.** Yes. Mid-market slice (ACV $8-25K, cycle 4-8 weeks), full ownership discovery through close, Jane keeps strategic accounts (> $50K enterprise) and the retained-existing-customer expansion motion.

Jane runs the AE hire against mod-110's rubric (not scoped in this chapter), hires by month 13, and executes the 60-90 day ramp plan.

**90-day review at month 16.** The AE (Sam) has closed 3 deals ($42K TCV combined), has 4 more in Proposal or Close (Commit + Best-case ~$70K), is running her pipeline reviews independently, has iterated the discovery script twice (added a specific question about GitHub Actions workflows that the previous script missed), and is on plan against quota. Jane's time-on-sales has dropped to 18% — reallocated to a security-review push for the next SOC 2 audit (product) and a Series A prep push (fundraising).

Hand-off worked. Pack is stronger than it was at month 12 because Sam has stress-tested it against 25 first meetings and 10 demos. The founder-led motion has completed its intended lifespan.

Contrast: had Jane hired in month 4 — before the pack was characterised, with only 6 wins in the CRM, no reason-code pattern, no substitution-tested pack — the AE would have arrived to a discovery script that lived in Jane's head, no rate card, a CRM with 3 fields, and Jane's frustration when the AE could not reproduce Jane's tacit process. That is the premature-hire pattern the diagnostic prevents.

## Common failure patterns

- **The premature-hire trap.** Founder hires an AE / VP Sales before the pack is characterised. Hire spends 6 months trying to reconstruct what the founder should have built; is fired at month 10; founder concludes "sales hiring is broken." Fix: five signals, all firing, before the hire.
- **The prolonged-founder-led trap.** Founder closes deal 30, 40, 60 personally; refuses to hire; plateaus at founder-calendar capacity. Fix: signals firing + founder-as-bottleneck signal is a trigger, not just a suggestion. Hire when the diagnostic fires, not when the co-founder or board forces it.
- **The wrong-role-first-hire trap.** Founder hires an SDR when the back-half is the bottleneck, or hires a VP Sales when a first AE is what fits. Fix: role decision matches the specific bottleneck the founder faces.
- **The candidate-availability-hire trap.** "A great candidate is on the market; I have to hire now." Availability of a candidate is not a diagnostic signal. Fix: hire against the pack readiness, not against the calendar of any one candidate.
- **The investor-pressure-hire trap.** Board or investor says "you need a VP Sales." Founder complies without running the diagnostic. Fix: walk the investor through the five signals; either the signals fire (proceed) or they don't (name what is missing and go build it).
- **The founder-tired-of-selling-hire trap.** Founder wants out of sales for personal reasons. Hire without ready pack fails; founder ends up back in sales anyway. Fix: characterise the pack first (with sales-advisor help if needed), hire second.
- **The no-substitution-test trap.** Founder skips Signal 5 — never runs the pack against a colleague — and assumes it will work with a hire. AE arrives, tries the pack, produces broken calls. Fix: 60-90 minutes of substitution test with a co-founder or advisor before the hire is scoped.
- **The no-ramp-plan trap.** AE arrives, founder says "read the pack, ask questions if you have any." AE ramps randomly, no shadowing, no supervised deals, no first-close target. Fix: written 60-90 day ramp plan, phased milestones, weekly 1:1 pipeline review.
- **The founder-doesnt-let-go trap.** AE is hired but founder is still on every call, every deal, every proposal. AE has no authority; hire is functionally not real. Fix: written founder-out plan; founder-time-on-sales drops from 40%+ to 15-20% by day 60.
- **The founder-disappears trap.** AE is hired and founder exits sales entirely — never on strategic accounts, never in the pipeline review, never in the reason-code rollup. Product / ICP / pricing feedback loop breaks. Fix: founder-out is *redistribution*, not exit. Weekly review, strategic accounts, monthly rollups remain founder work.
- **The no-pack-iteration trap.** AE runs the pack unchanged for 6 months; new signal from the field never lands in the pack. Fix: AE is expected to propose pack edits; founder reviews weekly; v-next ships monthly.
- **The no-90-day-review trap.** Founder hires and hopes. Six months in, motion is stagnant; nobody has diagnosed why. Fix: explicit 90-day review against the three review questions (pack-shaped behaviour, iteration, founder-out reallocation).
- **The signal-flat-revenue-conflation trap.** Founder reads a good revenue quarter as a "ready to hire" signal. Fix: revenue is a lagging indicator; the five characterisation signals are the leading indicators.
- **The wrong-slice trap.** AE is given the entire pipeline (SMB through enterprise, all segments). Cannot focus, cannot specialise, cannot generate a pattern. Fix: AE gets a defined slice (typically mid-market by ACV); founder keeps strategic-account slice.
- **The unwritten founder-out trap.** Founder-out lives in the founder's head; AE does not know which accounts are hers. Deals get double-worked or dropped. Fix: written strategic-account list; ACV threshold in writing; product-tied-deal rule in writing.

## Summary

- The founder-led motion has an intended lifespan: it exists to close the first 20-30 customers by hand and produce the pack a first hire can inherit. The **hand-off is a characterised transition**, not an event.
- **Five readiness signals** — all must fire: (1) repeatable discovery script, (2) repeatable win pattern, (3) repeatable loss pattern with disqualifier discipline, (4) founder as measurable bottleneck, (5) pack passes the substitution test against a colleague running it live.
- **Four false-readiness signals** to distrust: a good revenue quarter, investor pressure, candidate availability, founder-tired-of-selling. External urgency ≠ pack readiness.
- **Role decision** — SDR (outbound-bottleneck only), first AE (default at SEED post-diagnostic), Head of Sales (rarely correct pre-Series A). Match the hire to the specific bottleneck.
- **60-90 day AE ramp plan** in three phases: Phase 1 (days 1-30) absorb pack + shadow; Phase 2 (days 31-60) run supervised deals + first close; Phase 3 (days 61-90) run unsupervised + iterate the pack.
- **Founder-out plan** — founder still owns: strategic-account list, above-threshold ACV deals, product-tied deals, weekly pipeline review, monthly reason-code rollup, pack iteration. Founder stops: routine discovery / demo / close on the AE's slice, CRM data entry, cold outreach on assigned segments, jumping into buyer threads unsolicited.
- **Two-month rule of thumb** — founder-time-on-sales drops from 40%+ to 15-20% by day 60 post-hire. If it hasn't, founder is under-delegating; if it drops below 5%, founder is over-delegating and losing the product / ICP feedback loop.
- **90-day review** — three questions: (1) pack-shaped behaviour, (2) pack iteration happening, (3) founder-out reallocation real. Failing one is diagnosable; failing two triggers a structured post-mortem.
- **Pack becomes stronger post-hire** — multi-voice, multi-user CRM, diverse reason codes. The founder's governance job is to reconcile voices and coding conventions, not to preserve her exact original wording.
- **Bridge to mod-110** — the pack is the input to mod-110's compensation, hiring rubric, ramp instrumentation, and org design. The five signals are the SEED-to-Series-A transition trigger.
- Chapter 8 closes the module. The **shippable pack** — outreach sequence + discovery script + demo checklist + next-step templates + proposal template + rate card + CRM stage definitions + first-hire readiness checklist + founder-out plan — is the module's cumulative output.
