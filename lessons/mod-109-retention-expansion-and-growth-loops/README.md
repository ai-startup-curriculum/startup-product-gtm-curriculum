# mod-109 — Retention, Expansion, and Growth Loops — Building for NRR

**Stage:** SERIES-A
**Pillar:** GTM
**Hours:** 22 (7 lecture chapters + 6 exercises + 1 lab)
**Track:** [Startup Product & GTM](../../CURRICULUM.md)

> *NRR compounds; churn compounds faster. Ship for retention before you ship for growth.*

## What this module trains

Take the diagnostic retention read from [mod-102](../mod-102-pmf-signal-and-measurement) and turn it into an **operating discipline for durability**. Read cohort retention curves as the primary signal that PMF is still alive. Separate **gross revenue retention (GRR — the churn floor)** from **net revenue retention (NRR — the churn-plus-expansion number)** and know why NRR ≥ 100% at Series-A is the modern benchmark that a public-market underwriter recognises as a scalable SaaS motion. Design an **expansion motion** — seat expansion, cross-sell into adjacent surface, usage-based upgrade — as a *first-order revenue lever* rather than a customer-success afterthought. Replace the AARRR funnel with **growth loops** (Balfour / Reforge) where the loop applies, and be honest when the product does not support one. Identify the **magic moment** — the action a retained user takes in the first N days — and design an **activation programme** around it. Diagnose **voluntary vs. involuntary (payment-failure) churn** separately, run **saved-vs.-lost** analysis, and stop treating churn as a single number.

The failure mode this module exists to catch: **the founder has PMF in one segment, ignores the fact that GRR is 78% and NRR is 92%, hires AEs on the assumption that growth compounds, discovers eighteen months later that the leaky bucket has eaten every additional dollar of CAC they spent, and cannot raise a Series-B off metrics that a public-market underwriter would score as sub-scale.**

The remedy this module ships: a **retention + expansion scorecard** with cohort retention curves, GRR, NRR, expansion motion definition, one growth loop the product supports (or an explicit call that it does not), one activation programme designed around the magic moment, and a churn-diagnosis programme that separates voluntary from involuntary.

## Learning objectives

- Read a cohort retention curve as the primary PMF durability signal — daily / weekly / monthly cohorts, flattening as the health signal, and the compounding cost of a leaky bucket on GTM CAC efficiency.
- Distinguish gross revenue retention (GRR — churn floor) from net revenue retention (NRR — churn plus expansion) — and know why NRR ≥ 100% at Series-A is the modern benchmark for a scalable SaaS motion.
- Design an expansion motion — seat expansion, cross-sell into adjacent product surface, usage-based upgrade paths — as a first-order revenue lever, not an afterthought.
- Replace the AARRR funnel with growth loops (Brian Balfour / Reforge) where the loop applies — viral loop, content loop, paid loop, sales-assisted loop — and diagnose which loop the product supports (or does not).
- Read the retention diagnostic Andrew Chen ships — retention curve, cohort behaviour, magic-moment identification (the action a retained user takes in the first N days) — and design an activation programme around it.
- Design a churn-diagnosis programme — voluntary vs. involuntary (payment-failure) churn, saved-vs.-lost analysis, exit surveys with signal — and stop treating churn as a single number.
- Ship a retention + expansion scorecard for a real (or hypothetical) startup — retention curve, GRR, NRR, expansion motion definition, one growth loop the product supports, one activation programme.
