# Resources — mod-106 Pricing and Packaging

Citable references the module is grounded in. Primary sources and canonical practitioner texts first; adjacent operational writing second. Every chapter cites at least one item on this list; every module-level claim about a named framework (Van Westendorp PSM, Gabor-Granger, Max-Diff, value-based pricing, good/better/best, compromise effect, grandfathering discipline) traces back to a primary source below.

## Willingness-to-pay research — the three instruments (Chapter 1)

**Van Westendorp Price-Sensitivity Meter (PSM).**

- **Peter van Westendorp — "NSS-Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM) — A new approach to study consumer perception of price."** ESOMAR Congress, Venice, 1976. The original PSM paper. The ESOMAR archive holds the proceedings; the paper is the canonical citation for PSM. ESOMAR: [https://www.esomar.org/](https://www.esomar.org/).
- **PSM overview and mechanics — subsequent practitioner literature.** Widely summarised in market-research textbooks; see the pricing-research literature listed under Nagle & Müller below for treatments consistent with the original.

**Gabor-Granger.**

- **André Gabor and Clive Granger — "Price Sensitivity of the Consumer."** *Journal of Advertising Research*, 4 (December 1964), pp. 40-44. The original paper. JAR archive: [https://www.journalofadvertisingresearch.com/](https://www.journalofadvertisingresearch.com/).
- **André Gabor — *Pricing: Concepts and Methods for Effective Marketing* (Gower, 1988; 2nd ed.).** The book-length treatment of the Gabor-Granger method, including sample-size guidance and interpretation cautions. Publisher: [https://www.routledge.com/Pricing-Concepts-and-Methods-for-Effective-Marketing/Gabor/p/book/9780566056611](https://www.routledge.com/Pricing-Concepts-and-Methods-for-Effective-Marketing/Gabor/p/book/9780566056611).
- **Clive Granger — Nobel Prize in Economics 2003.** Autobiographical note including references to the Gabor collaboration: [https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2003/granger/biographical/](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2003/granger/biographical/).

**Max-Diff (best-worst scaling).**

- **Jordan Louviere, Terry Flynn, and A. A. J. Marley — *Best-Worst Scaling: Theory, Methods and Applications* (Cambridge University Press, 2015).** The comprehensive practitioner + academic treatment of Max-Diff. Cambridge page: [https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bestworst-scaling/8FF37437FB4C43FF9DBDF7181CB5AC28](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bestworst-scaling/8FF37437FB4C43FF9DBDF7181CB5AC28).
- **Jordan Louviere — foundational best-worst scaling papers from the late 1980s and 1990s.** See the reference list in the 2015 book for the primary papers.
- **Sawtooth Software — Max-Diff technical papers.** Sawtooth is the dominant industry tool for choice-based experimental design including Max-Diff; their technical library documents implementation and interpretation. [https://sawtoothsoftware.com/resources](https://sawtoothsoftware.com/resources).

## Value-based pricing — the canon (Chapter 2)

- **Thomas T. Nagle and Georg Müller — *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing: A Guide to Growing More Profitably* (Routledge, 6th ed. 2017; earlier editions with Nagle and Holden).** The definitive practitioner text on value-based pricing, the value / cost / competitor triangle, and value-capture strategy. The three-anchor frame in Chapters 1 and 2 traces here. Routledge: [https://www.routledge.com/The-Strategy-and-Tactics-of-Pricing-A-Guide-to-Growing-More-Profitably/Nagle-Muller/p/book/9781138737518](https://www.routledge.com/The-Strategy-and-Tactics-of-Pricing-A-Guide-to-Growing-More-Profitably/Nagle-Muller/p/book/9781138737518).
- **Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke — *Monetizing Innovation: How Smart Companies Design the Product Around the Price* (Wiley, 2016).** The Simon-Kucher-anchored practitioner text on designing product + pricing together. The 10-25% capture-rule-of-thumb referenced in Chapter 2 is consistent with Simon-Kucher's published writing. Wiley: [https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Monetizing+Innovation%3A+How+Smart+Companies+Design+the+Product+Around+the+Price-p-9781119240860](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Monetizing+Innovation%3A+How+Smart+Companies+Design+the+Product+Around+the+Price-p-9781119240860).
- **Simon-Kucher & Partners — pricing research and industry reports.** Long-running consultancy with an extensive published body of work on B2B software pricing, tier design, and value-based derivation. [https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights).
- **Hermann Simon — *Confessions of the Pricing Man: How Price Affects Everything* (Springer, 2015).** Simon-Kucher founder's memoir and pricing-strategy overview; useful complement to Nagle & Müller. Springer: [https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-20400-0](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-20400-0).

## Packaging + choice psychology (Chapter 3)

- **Itamar Simonson — "Choice Based on Reasons: The Case of Attraction and Compromise Effects."** *Journal of Consumer Research*, 16 (September 1989), pp. 158-174. The foundational paper on the compromise effect that underpins the three-tier packaging pattern. JCR: [https://academic.oup.com/jcr](https://academic.oup.com/jcr).
- **Itamar Simonson and Amos Tversky — "Choice in Context: Tradeoff Contrast and Extremeness Aversion."** *Journal of Marketing Research*, 29 (August 1992), pp. 281-295. Follow-on paper extending the compromise-effect frame to explain extremeness aversion. JMR: [https://journals.sagepub.com/home/mrj](https://journals.sagepub.com/home/mrj).
- **Dan Ariely — *Predictably Irrational: The Hidden Forces That Shape Our Decisions* (HarperCollins, 2008; revised 2010).** Popular treatment of the *Economist*-subscription three-option experiment (the canonical illustration of the decoy / anchor effect used in packaging). HarperCollins: [https://www.harpercollins.com/products/predictably-irrational-revised-and-expanded-edition-dan-ariely](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/predictably-irrational-revised-and-expanded-edition-dan-ariely).
- **Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein — *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness* (Yale University Press, 2008; final edition 2021).** Broader treatment of choice architecture that frames the packaging decision as a designed default rather than a neutral menu. Publisher: [https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300262285/nudge/](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300262285/nudge/).

## Suggested reading order for this module

If starting cold:

1. Read [Thomas Nagle and Georg Müller — *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing*](https://www.routledge.com/The-Strategy-and-Tactics-of-Pricing-A-Guide-to-Growing-More-Profitably/Nagle-Muller/p/book/9781138737518) Chapters 1-4 on the value / cost / competitor triangle (~3 hr).
2. Read [Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke — *Monetizing Innovation*](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Monetizing+Innovation%3A+How+Smart+Companies+Design+the+Product+Around+the+Price-p-9781119240860) Parts 1-2 on price-anchored product design and the 10-25% capture band (~3 hr).
3. Skim [Louviere, Flynn, Marley — *Best-Worst Scaling*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bestworst-scaling/8FF37437FB4C43FF9DBDF7181CB5AC28) Chapters 1-2 for the Max-Diff mechanics (~1 hr).
4. Read Simonson's ["Choice Based on Reasons"](https://academic.oup.com/jcr) 1989 JCR paper for the compromise-effect derivation (~1 hr).
5. Skim [Dan Ariely — *Predictably Irrational*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/predictably-irrational-revised-and-expanded-edition-dan-ariely) chapters on the *Economist*-subscription decoy (~30 min).
6. Skim [Geoffrey Moore — *Crossing the Chasm*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/crossing-the-chasm-3rd-edition-geoffrey-a-moore) Chapters 1-4 for the mainstream-adoption frame that constrains value-based pricing (~1 hr).
7. Read the OpenView / Kyle Poyar usage-based-pricing archive posts on [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/) (~1 hr).
8. Skim [David Skok — SaaS Metrics 2.0](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/) for cohort-analysis mechanics (~1 hr).

Total: ~11 hours of prerequisite reading before authoring a defensible pricing + packaging pack against the exercises.
