# Exercise 06 — Hand-Recruit the First Ten Drill

**Estimated time:** 3 hours of drill + ongoing calendar time to actually reach ten
**Chapter link:** [`03-do-things-that-dont-scale.md`](../03-do-things-that-dont-scale.md)

## Problem statement

Chapter 3 argued that at n=10, **manual recruiting beats every scalable path** — personal network first, then warm referrals, then communities, then cold direct outreach, then paid panels only as a last resort. This exercise makes you do that ranking against your real network, write the messages you would actually send, and — the un-optional part — send them.

At the end of the exercise, you will have a hand-recruit plan for ten interviews *and* you will have sent at least ten first-touch messages against it. This exercise is where a lot of founders discover their real distribution constraint: the recruiting funnel is where the reachability problem shows up before the discovery problem does.

## Requirements

Deliver a single markdown file `exercise-06-hand-recruit.md` under your working folder, plus **evidence of the outreach actually sent** (see step 5).

### 1. ICP-fit reachable-list — 45 minutes

Enumerate every person in your network who plausibly fits the ICP from Exercise 02. Include:

- Former colleagues at ICP-fit companies.
- Ex-classmates or bootcamp cohort-mates in ICP-fit roles.
- Anyone in the co-founder's, advisor's, or investor's network who might fit (and whom you could ask them to intro).
- People you follow / who follow you on the relevant platforms (LinkedIn, Twitter/X, GitHub, industry Slacks) who fit.

Deliver as a table:

| Name | Role / company / segment | Channel to reach (personal / warm intro from X / community Y / cold) | ICP fit (high / medium / low) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Target: **≥ 20 candidates** even if not all are high-fit. If you cannot list 20, note that as a distribution finding in the reflection (step 6).

### 2. Rank and select — 15 minutes

From the list, select the **first ten you will contact**, prioritised by Chapter 3's channel ranking: exhaust personal network first, then warm referrals, then community, then cold. Do not select the ten easiest — select the ten highest-fit reachable candidates in channel-rank order.

For each of the ten, note the channel and the reasoning for the selection.

### 3. Write the ten first-touch messages — 60 minutes

Draft the actual message you will send to each of the ten. **Do not use a single template for all ten**; a message to a former colleague reads differently from a warm-intro request from an advisor which reads differently from a community post which reads differently from a cold ask.

Every message must satisfy the Chapter 3 ask criteria:

- Specific ICP language (why *they* were asked).
- Specific workflow (what the conversation is about).
- Explicit "not selling anything" disclaimer where applicable.
- Bounded time ask (20 minutes).
- A concrete return (share the aggregated pattern, share the deck, share the prototype).

Include the referral-ask variant for post-interview follow-up as a separate section — the message you will send *after* an interview to compound the network.

### 4. Community post — 15 minutes

Draft one **community-channel post** for a public community (Slack, subreddit, LinkedIn Group, Discord) where the ICP hangs out. It should be one message that respects the community rules (read them first; if the community forbids research asks, note which alternative community you would use instead). Include:

- Which community you would post in and why it fits the ICP.
- The community's rules on research asks (link them or quote them if published).
- The post itself, ready to send.

### 5. Send at least ten first-touch messages — evidence required

**This is the non-negotiable part of the exercise.** Actually send the messages. Deliver a `outreach-log.md` file that records, for each of the ten:

- Whom you sent to.
- Which channel.
- The timestamp of the send.
- The response (yes / no / no response) at the moment of exercise submission.
- If yes, whether you scheduled a call and when.

You may redact names for privacy (use pseudonyms or initials) but the count, channels, and response pattern must be honest.

Target: **≥ 3 scheduled calls** within one week of sending. If you get fewer, that is the distribution finding this exercise exists to surface.

### 6. Reflection — 30 minutes

A one-page memo covering:

- **Reachability audit.** How large is the ICP-fit reachable pool? Is it big enough to sustain 20–30 interviews across the module and project?
- **Channel effectiveness so far.** Which channel produced the highest response rate in this first batch? Which produced the highest-quality yes?
- **The friction you felt.** Which asks felt awkward to send? What was the pattern (asking friends is awkward, cold outreach felt like spam, community post felt self-promoting)? Chapter 3 predicts that the friction is often what pushes founders to inferior scalable options — did you feel that pressure?
- **What you would change** for the next ten (interviews 11–20).
- **A no-strangers-can-hear-me admission.** If your reachable pool for this ICP is thin — five candidates, not fifty — that is a real signal about whether you have chosen a segment you have any right to sell to. Write about it honestly.

## Starter guidance

- The exercise's design is intentionally uncomfortable. Sending asks to your network at the "researching X" stage feels awkward the first time. Chapter 3 argues the awkwardness is the un-scalable thing and it is the whole point. Do it anyway.
- Warm intros compound. Every "yes" you get from steps 1–5 should generate a referral ask in step 5's response follow-up — that is what turns 10 first-touch messages into 25 interviews scheduled.
- The community post (step 4) is often the highest-leverage single message. A good one in the right community can produce 5–10 responses. Read the community rules carefully — a message posted against the rules is worse than no post.
- Paid panels (User Interviews, Respondent, etc.) are explicitly deprioritised for this exercise. If you feel the pull to jump straight to paid, that is worth noting in the reflection — the pull is the exact failure mode Chapter 3 warns against.
- Screen every "yes" before you schedule. One clarifying question ("just to confirm — you are running 30+ invoices a month, is that right?") saves a wasted call.

## Acceptance criteria

Your submission is complete when:

- [ ] The reachable-list table has ≥ 20 candidates with ICP-fit ratings.
- [ ] The ten selected are prioritised in Chapter 3's channel-rank order with per-selection reasoning.
- [ ] All ten drafted messages exist; each is customised per channel; each satisfies the five Chapter 3 ask criteria; a separate referral-ask template is included.
- [ ] The community post identifies the community, cites its rules on research asks, and is ready to send.
- [ ] `outreach-log.md` documents ≥ 10 real messages sent, with channels, timestamps, and responses recorded honestly.
- [ ] The reflection covers reachability, channel effectiveness, the friction pattern, changes for the next ten, and an honest read on whether the reachable pool sustains the module's 20-interview target.

## Common ways this exercise goes wrong

- **Drafting messages but not sending them.** The exercise's whole design point is the send. A locked drawer of unsent asks is a way to avoid the work.
- **One template for all ten.** A friend does not need the "not selling anything" disclaimer; a cold LinkedIn contact absolutely does. Customise.
- **Community post that violates the community rules.** You will burn the community and worse — you will get taste-blamed for it. Read the rules; if unsure, ask a mod first.
- **Skipping the referral-ask template.** Compound is where the 10 becomes 20 becomes 40. If you never ask for referrals, every batch is the same size as the first.
- **Reflection that does not admit a thin pool.** If your reachable ICP-fit pool is 5 people, that is a segment-selection finding and you should write about it honestly. It is more valuable than a polite reflection that hides the constraint.
