# Exercise 02 — Problem Interview Script v0

**Estimated time:** 2 hours
**Chapter links:** [`01-customer-development-loop.md`](../01-customer-development-loop.md), [`02-mom-test-discipline.md`](../02-mom-test-discipline.md), [`04-jobs-to-be-done-outcomes.md`](../04-jobs-to-be-done-outcomes.md)

## Problem statement

Author a v0 problem-interview script for one specific startup and one specific ICP. This script is what you will run against real people in Exercise 03; the quality of the interviews you run next week depends on the discipline of the script you write today.

The v0 script must be short enough to fit in a 20–30 minute interview, Mom-Test-compliant end-to-end, and structured to surface the three things a problem interview exists to surface: **current-state workflow, pain and recency, and switch-story forces**.

## Requirements

Deliver as a single markdown file `exercise-02-script.md` under your working folder.

### 1. ICP and hypothesis header

At the top of the script, state:

- **The startup** — real (name it) or hypothetical (describe it in one sentence). If hypothetical, this is the startup you will use for the rest of the module.
- **The ICP for this interview** — role, company shape, and one behavioural qualifier. (Example: *"Engineering managers at 50–200-person companies with distributed teams across ≥3 time zones."*)
- **The problem hypothesis** — one sentence, in the form *"we believe [ICP] have [problem] when [situation], and today they [current workaround], at a cost of [magnitude]."*
- **The three things the script must surface** — pick from Chapter 4's job / outcome / force framework and name which three you are prioritising in this v0.

### 2. The script — five sections, ~20-30 minutes total

Write the script as a bulleted question sequence, section by section, with **estimated minutes per section** noted. Every question must be Mom-Test-compliant. Constraints:

- **(2–3 min) Warm-up / consent.** How you introduce yourself, why they were asked, the ask for permission to record, an explicit "this is not a sales call".
- **(5–7 min) Current-state workflow.** Past-tense specific questions about how the interviewee handles the workflow today. Include the ask for a **walk-through of the last specific instance** (not a general summary).
- **(5–7 min) Pain, recency, and cost.** The three Mom Test diagnostics from Chapter 2 — when was the last time, what did they do about it, what did it cost.
- **(5–7 min) Switch-story / forces (Chapter 4).** Ask about the last time they changed tools or workflows in this space — push, pull, anxiety, habit. Even if they have not switched, ask about the last time they *considered* it and did not.
- **(2–3 min) Commitment ask + referrals.** A concrete commitment-ladder ask appropriate to a first-round problem interview (Chapter 2: follow-up permission, referrals, prototype-session ask). At least two referral asks phrased differently.

### 3. Kill-list — the questions you deliberately did *not* include

At the bottom of the script, list **at least five founder-fishing questions you considered including and rejected**, with a one-line reason for each rejection (e.g., "*would you use a Slack bot for this*" — rejected: future-hypothetical, will produce compliment, replaced by past-tense workflow question in section 2).

This list forces you to prove you can spot the anti-patterns from Chapter 2 in your own draft, not just in a diagnostic quiz.

### 4. Script-length sanity check

State the estimated total interview length. If it is over 30 minutes, cut. Discovery interviews at the IDEA stage should not run longer than 30 minutes — the ask is unreasonable and information density falls off past that.

## Starter guidance

- Draft the script section by section, not top-to-bottom. Start with the middle (pain / recency / cost) — that is the section that carries the most information — then wrap the warm-up and the commitment ask around it.
- **Read each question aloud** before you commit it. Founder-fishing questions almost always sound obvious when spoken; they hide when read silently.
- Every question in sections 2–4 must be **specific and past-tense**. If a question could be answered in the abstract, rewrite it to demand a specific instance.
- Do not include a slide deck, a screenshot, or any artifact of your solution. A problem interview has no artifact. Solution interviews are Chapter 5 territory.
- The commitment ask should be a **real ask**, not a script prop — write it as if you were going to say it out loud to a real person on Monday.

## Acceptance criteria

Your submission is complete when:

- [ ] The header names a specific startup, a specific ICP, a specific problem hypothesis, and the three things the script must surface.
- [ ] All five script sections are present with estimated minutes; total length ≤ 30 minutes.
- [ ] Every question in sections 2–4 is past-tense and specific; nothing uses **would / could / might / imagine / if / hypothetically**.
- [ ] The switch-story section includes explicit ask for at least two of the four forces (push, pull, anxiety, habit).
- [ ] The commitment ask is a real, concrete ask; there are at least two referral asks phrased differently.
- [ ] The kill-list has at least 5 rejected founder-fishing questions with per-line rejection reasons.
- [ ] The script fits on ≤ 2 pages when printed — you could take it into an interview and read it as-is.

## Common ways this exercise goes wrong

- **The script pitches in the warm-up.** "Hi, I'm working on a tool that does X, wanted to get your feedback" — you have just biased every answer that follows. The warm-up should describe the *workflow* you are researching, not your product.
- **Section 2 asks for opinions instead of walk-throughs.** "How do you handle code reviews in general?" gets a summary. "Walk me through the last PR you reviewed — when was it, what did you do first?" gets a transcript.
- **No switch-story section.** Without it, you miss the forces framework entirely; you have a competent problem interview but not one that will generate ICP-level insight for Chapter 4.
- **Commitment ask that is not a real ask.** "Would you be interested in learning more?" is a script prop, not a commitment. Ask for something real.
- **Total length > 30 minutes.** Interviewees will drop out, you will get worse referrals, and the last 15 minutes will fall off in signal quality. Cut.
