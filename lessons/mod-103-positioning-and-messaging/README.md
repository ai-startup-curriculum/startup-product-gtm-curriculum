# mod-103 — Positioning and Messaging

**Stage:** SEED
**Pillar:** GTM
**Hours:** 24 (7 lecture chapters + 6 exercises + 1 lab)
**Track:** [Startup Product & GTM](../../CURRICULUM.md)

> *Positioning is the choice; messaging is the words that carry it.*

## What this module trains

Author a defensible **five-component positioning** in the [April Dunford — *Obviously Awesome*](https://www.aprildunford.com/obviously-awesome) frame (competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → best-for characteristics → market category), diagnose the **three positioning failure modes** (obvious-but-underwhelming, "we're better", category confusion) on real product pages, ship a **strategic-narrative deck** in the [Andy Raskin](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0) six-part shape (shift → enemy → promised land → evidence → obstacles → tools), and pick a defensible **beachhead segment** in the [Geoffrey Moore — *Crossing the Chasm*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/crossing-the-chasm-3rd-edition-geoffrey-a-moore) tradition. Ship a positioning document + narrative deck + revised product-page hero for a real (or hypothetical) startup, and pressure-test the positioning against three named competitive alternatives.

The failure mode this module exists to catch: **the founder rewrites the tagline every quarter, hoping better words will fix a funnel that is actually starving on broken positioning; nine months of copy iteration and A/B tests move nothing, because the positioning underneath the words is compared against the wrong alternatives, filed under the wrong category, and pitched to a buyer who cannot tell which mental shelf the product belongs on.**

## Learning objectives

- Author a positioning statement using the April Dunford five-component frame — competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value (derived from attributes), best-for characteristics, market category — and defend each component against the failure mode of "default positioning".
- Distinguish positioning (a strategic choice about which context the product is best in) from messaging (the words a buyer sees) — positioning changes rarely and structurally; messaging tests weekly.
- Diagnose the three positioning failure modes Dunford names — obvious-but-underwhelming (positioned against the wrong alternatives), "we're better" (unique attributes without value), and "category confusion" (buyer doesn't know which mental shelf to put you on) — on real product pages.
- Ship a strategic-narrative deck in the Andy Raskin shape — name the shift, name the enemy, promised land, evidence, obstacles, tools — and know why a founder narrative structured this way is more resilient than a product-feature deck.
- Use the Moore Crossing-the-Chasm beachhead frame to pick the first defensible segment to position against — early-adopter validation now, mainstream reference-buyer path later.
- Ship a positioning document + narrative deck + revised product-page headline for a real (or hypothetical) startup, and pressure-test the positioning against three named competitive alternatives.

## How this module connects to the rest of the track

- **Fed by ← [mod-101](../mod-101-customer-discovery-and-problem-validation)** — the problem statement + top jobs + top forces from the discovery report are the raw material for the Dunford Component 1 (alternatives) and Component 4 (best-for characteristics). Positioning that skips the discovery corpus is founder-invented.
- **Fed by ← [mod-102](../mod-102-pmf-signal-and-measurement)** — the very-disappointed cohort's main-benefit statement is the raw material for the Component 3 (value) authoring; the who-is-this-for answer sharpens Component 4 (best-for). A positioning that contradicts the surveyed main benefit is a positioning that ignores the PMF signal.
- **Feeds → [mod-104](../mod-104-icp-and-buyer-personas)** — the Chapter 2 best-for characteristics and the Chapter 5 beachhead segment become the seed of the scoreable ICP + disqualification checklist. An ICP contradicting the positioning is broken.
- **Feeds → [mod-105](../mod-105-founder-led-sales)** — the Chapter 4 strategic-narrative deck becomes the founder's sales pitch; discovery-call script, demo structure, and objection-handling all derive from the positioning's value chains.
- **Feeds → [mod-106](../mod-106-pricing-and-packaging)** — the Chapter 2 Component 5 (market category) is the primary price anchor and the Chapter 5 beachhead segment's willingness-to-pay is the primary pricing input. Pricing that contradicts the category's price band collides with buyer expectation.
- **Feeds → [mod-107](../mod-107-sales-motion-design)** — the best-for characteristics + beachhead + category determine motion (PLG / SDR-AE / enterprise-MEDDPICC). The motion follows the beachhead, not the founder's preference.
- **Feeds → [mod-108](../mod-108-demand-generation-and-channels)** — the beachhead's peer-community locations (from the Chapter 5 reference-able criterion) are the primary channel candidates. Channel-market fit is beachhead-specific.
- **Feeds → [`project-101-first-thirty-paying-customers`](../../projects/project-101-first-thirty-paying-customers)** — the beachhead + narrative become the primary sales-conversation inputs.
- **Feeds → [`project-102-positioning-and-gtm-plan`](../../projects/project-102-positioning-and-gtm-plan)** — the whole three-artifact ship (positioning doc + narrative deck + revised hero) is the project's core, extended with a demand-gen plan, a motion-design doc, and a 30-60-90 execution plan.
