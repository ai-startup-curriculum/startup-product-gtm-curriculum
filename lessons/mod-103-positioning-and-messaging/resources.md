# Resources — mod-103 Positioning and Messaging

Citable references the module is grounded in. Primary sources first; adjacent practitioner writing second. Every chapter cites at least one item on this list.

## Positioning — April Dunford

- **April Dunford — *Obviously Awesome: How to nail product positioning so customers get it, buy it, love it* (2019).** The canonical text this module is built on. Book site: [https://www.aprildunford.com/obviously-awesome](https://www.aprildunford.com/obviously-awesome).
- **April Dunford — *Sales Pitch: How to Craft a Story to Stand Out and Win* (2023).** Follow-on that connects positioning to the sales-pitch derivation. Book site: [https://www.aprildunford.com/sales-pitch](https://www.aprildunford.com/sales-pitch).
- **April Dunford — blog and essay archive.** Includes "The 5 Building Blocks of Positioning", "The three styles of positioning", and shorter posts that re-state the frame across contexts. [https://www.aprildunford.com/blog](https://www.aprildunford.com/blog).
- **April Dunford — podcast interviews and conference talks.** Multiple long-form interviews (Lenny's Podcast, First Round Review, SaaStr) walk the five-component frame end-to-end. Search "April Dunford positioning podcast" for current listings.

## Strategic narrative — Andy Raskin

- **Andy Raskin — "The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen" (2016).** The Medium essay teardown of Zuora's sales deck that codified the six-part shape. Read end-to-end. [https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0).
- **Andy Raskin — writing archive.** Follow-on essays that extend the strategic-narrative pattern to fundraising decks, All-Hands, and CEO writing. [https://www.andyraskin.com/writing](https://www.andyraskin.com/writing).
- **Andy Raskin — workshop and consulting site.** [https://www.andyraskin.com/](https://www.andyraskin.com/).

## Crossing the Chasm — Geoffrey Moore

- **Geoffrey Moore — *Crossing the Chasm: Marketing and Selling Disruptive Products to Mainstream Customers* (1991, revised 2014).** The founding text for the beachhead / bowling-pin strategy. Publisher page: [https://www.harpercollins.com/products/crossing-the-chasm-3rd-edition-geoffrey-a-moore](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/crossing-the-chasm-3rd-edition-geoffrey-a-moore).
- **Geoffrey Moore — *Inside the Tornado* (1995).** The sequel that extends the beachhead metaphor with the bowling-pin adjacency model and the tornado phase. [https://www.harpercollins.com/products/inside-the-tornado-geoffrey-a-moore](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/inside-the-tornado-geoffrey-a-moore).
- **Geoffrey Moore — *Zone to Win* (2015).** Later book on portfolio-level management of the adoption lifecycle inside larger companies; adjacent but useful for the "what happens after chasm crossing" question. [https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/books](https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/books).
- **Geoffrey Moore — author site and speaking archive.** [https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/](https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/).
- **Everett Rogers — *Diffusion of Innovations* (1962; 5th edition 2003).** The underlying adoption-lifecycle model Moore built on. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_of_Innovations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_of_Innovations); publisher page: [https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Diffusion-of-Innovations-5th-Edition/Everett-M-Rogers/9780743222099](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Diffusion-of-Innovations-5th-Edition/Everett-M-Rogers/9780743222099).

## Category design — the alternative-to-invention discipline

- **Play Bigger — *Play Bigger: How Pirates, Dreamers, and Innovators Create and Dominate Markets* (2016, Al Ramadan, Dave Peterson, Christopher Lochhead, Kevin Maney).** The canonical text on deliberate category design. Referenced in Chapter 2 as the "if you're going to invent a category, budget it as a multi-year, capital-intensive campaign" argument. [https://www.playbigger.com/](https://www.playbigger.com/).
- **Christopher Lochhead — *Niche Down* (2018) and podcast archive.** Practitioner writing on category-design mechanics. [https://christopherlochhead.com/](https://christopherlochhead.com/).

## Positioning-adjacent primary sources referenced in chapters

- **Al Ries and Jack Trout — *Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind* (1981).** The original book on positioning as a marketing discipline; predates Dunford by nearly forty years. Useful for the deep vocabulary; Dunford's frame is more operational for early-stage. [https://www.harpercollins.com/products/positioning-the-battle-for-your-mind-20th-anniversary-edition-al-riesjack-trout](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/positioning-the-battle-for-your-mind-20th-anniversary-edition-al-riesjack-trout).
- **Michael Porter — *Competitive Strategy* (1980).** The five-forces / generic-strategies frame; adjacent to positioning at the corporate-strategy level. Useful for understanding where "differentiation vs. cost leadership" sits relative to Dunford's product-level positioning. [https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=195](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=195).
- **W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne — *Blue Ocean Strategy* (2005).** The "create uncontested market space" argument; adjacent to Dunford's category-shift move. [https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/).

## DORA and engineering-throughput references (used in Loomly running example)

- **DORA — State of DevOps reports.** The peer-reviewed research programme (now hosted at Google Cloud) that established the four DORA metrics — deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate, mean time to recovery — as the operating vocabulary for engineering throughput. Cited in the running Loomly example. [https://dora.dev/](https://dora.dev/); annual reports at [https://cloud.google.com/devops/state-of-devops](https://cloud.google.com/devops/state-of-devops).
- **Titus Winters, Tom Manshreck, Hyrum Wright (eds.) — *Software Engineering at Google* (2020).** O'Reilly book on Google's engineering practices, including the internal review-automation tooling referenced in the Loomly narrative example. Free online: [https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book).
- **GitHub — CODEOWNERS documentation.** [https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-code-owners](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-code-owners).

## Suggested reading order for this module

If starting cold:

1. Skim [Andy Raskin — "The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen"](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0) — the six-part narrative shape (~20 min).
2. Read [April Dunford — *Obviously Awesome*](https://www.aprildunford.com/obviously-awesome), Chapters 1-6 — the five-component frame + the three failure modes (~2 hr).
3. Read [Geoffrey Moore — *Crossing the Chasm*](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/crossing-the-chasm-3rd-edition-geoffrey-a-moore), Introduction + Chapter 3 (the chasm) + Chapter 4 (the D-Day analogy / beachhead) (~2 hr).
4. Skim [April Dunford — *Sales Pitch*](https://www.aprildunford.com/sales-pitch) — the messaging derivation half (~1 hr).
5. Read [Play Bigger](https://www.playbigger.com/) Chapter 1 for the category-design bar — mostly to understand when *not* to invent (~30 min).
6. Skim two or three of [Dunford's blog posts](https://www.aprildunford.com/blog) for the shorter re-statements of the frame (~30 min).

Total: ~6 hours of prerequisite reading before authoring a defensible positioning against the exercises.
