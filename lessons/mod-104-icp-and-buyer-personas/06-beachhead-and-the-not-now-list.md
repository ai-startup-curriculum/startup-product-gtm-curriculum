# Beachhead-Narrowed ICP — One Segment to Say Yes To, Three to Say No To

## Motivation

mod-103 Chapter 5 introduced Moore's beachhead-segment concept — the single narrow segment a startup commits to winning first, generating the concentrated reference pattern that lets it cross the chasm. That chapter framed the beachhead at the strategic-positioning altitude. This chapter operationalises the beachhead at the ICP altitude: the ICP artifact explicitly names the *one* segment it says yes to *and the three segments it says no to now*, with the not-now segments carrying an unlock condition and a nurture posture.

The failure mode this chapter exists to catch: **the ICP passes the Chapter 1-5 disciplines cleanly, but nothing in the artifact names the segments the founder has committed to not-serve, and by month four the AE is running deals in four segments simultaneously because "this one came in and it seemed too good to pass up."** The beachhead choice made in mod-103's positioning workshop is silently un-made deal by deal in the ICP execution.

The remedy is that the ICP artifact carries the beachhead commitment *inside itself*, in a named "not now" section that is as first-class as the positive criteria. The section carries specific segment names, specific reason codes, and specific unlock conditions. The AE looks at the "not now" section on every lead and enforces it. The founder reviews the "not now" list quarterly to decide whether any of the unlock conditions have tripped. The three said-no-to segments are the working set that mod-107 (motion design) and mod-108 (channel selection) revisit when the beachhead has been won.

This chapter connects the mod-103 beachhead frame to the mod-104 ICP artifact, names the three-segment discipline (one yes, three explicit nos), and specifies how the said-no-to list gets handed to mod-107 / mod-108 as the working input for the next stage.

## Core concepts

### The beachhead choice is a segment-level commitment; the ICP is the enforcement instrument

Recall the mod-103 hierarchy:

```
mod-103 best-for characteristics (Dunford Component 4)  — the widest space
  → mod-103 beachhead segment  — the narrower first-slice commitment
    → mod-104 ICP  — the scoreable filter inside the beachhead
```

The beachhead choice is made in the positioning workshop. It is an *upstream* decision. But the beachhead has no operational teeth without an enforcement instrument, and the ICP is that instrument. Chapter 3's disqualifier D4 (beachhead-scope disqualifier) is one lever; the "not now" section in this chapter is the other. Together they enforce at the individual-deal level the commitment the founder made at the positioning-workshop level.

Without the ICP-level enforcement, the beachhead commitment lasts about six weeks — until the first attractive out-of-beachhead deal appears — and then dissolves silently.

### The three-nos discipline

The pattern the module's title names: **one segment to say yes to; three segments to say no to now.** The "three" is a working number, not a magic one. In practice, the ICP author names 2-5 explicit no-segments. The specific number depends on how many adjacent-but-different segments the product could plausibly serve.

The categories the three-nos typically fall into:

1. **The obvious adjacent segment.** The segment structurally similar to the beachhead that a founder is most likely to slip into serving. Loomly's beachhead is B2B SaaS devtools mid-market; the obvious adjacent no is B2B SaaS fintech mid-market. Same size, same shape, same product surface — but different peer community (fintech engineers hang out in different Slack channels, different conferences, different Twitter followers), which means the reference pattern from Loomly's devtools wins does not carry to fintech.
2. **The larger segment the founder aspirationally wants.** Almost always enterprise. The founder's pitch deck names enterprise as "future revenue" and the enterprise deals are seductive because the ACV is 10× the mid-market. But enterprise has a whole-product gap, a different sales motion, and a different buying process — and every enterprise deal at SEED absorbs 3× the founder-hours of a mid-market deal at 10× the risk.
3. **The smaller segment the founder emotionally wants to serve.** Often the individual practitioner, the OSS-community, or the SMB below the ICP threshold. Sympathetic buyers, "we're solving their pain too." Usually too small an ACV to sustain a sales motion; usually can't reference into the beachhead segment.
4. **The horizontal-adjacent product play.** The temptation to "make the product also work for X use case" — a horizontal expansion that dilutes the positioning. Loomly-for-code-review nudging expanding into Loomly-for-general-workflow nudging is a horizontal expansion, not a segment expansion. The horizontal play is a positioning question (mod-103), not just a segmentation one.

Naming these three-to-four nos explicitly forces the founder to write down what the beachhead choice is *excluding*. The exercise is uncomfortable because each no is a real revenue opportunity being deliberately declined. But without writing them down, the exclusion is not binding.

### The "not now" section of the ICP artifact

Chapter 7's one-page ICP scorecard has a section titled **"Not now — segments we are deliberately not serving in the current beachhead"**. Format:

```
Not-now segment 1: {name}
  What it looks like: {2-3 firmographic + behavioural sentences}
  Why not now: {1 sentence — whole-product gap, motion mismatch, beachhead-adjacency risk}
  Unlock condition: {specific triggering event that lets us revisit — e.g., "Enterprise SSO + SCIM ships"}
  Meanwhile: {what to do when a lead in this segment appears — refer, downgrade, waitlist, close-lost}
  Revisit trigger: {who monitors the unlock condition, at what cadence}

Not-now segment 2: {name}
  ...

Not-now segment 3: {name}
  ...
```

Every not-now segment carries these five fields. The pattern the fields enforce:

- **What it looks like** — so the AE can *recognise* a not-now-segment lead when it arrives. Without a description, the AE mistakes an enterprise lead for a mid-market lead until three weeks in.
- **Why not now** — so the reason is written down and can be re-evaluated. "Because the founder said so" is not a reason; "because we do not have SSO / SCIM and enterprise procurement will not sign without it" is.
- **Unlock condition** — the specific triggering event that changes the calculation. Written prospectively; makes the "not now" a *conditional* rather than a *permanent* commitment.
- **Meanwhile** — the specific action to take on incoming leads in the segment. This is the AE's operational instruction; without it, the AE improvises and re-drifts.
- **Revisit trigger** — the ownership + cadence for monitoring. Usually a quarterly founder review; sometimes an automatic trigger (e.g., when the whole-product surface ships).

### Meanwhile actions — what to do with an out-of-beachhead lead

The ICP's "not now" section spells out the specific meanwhile action per segment. The four common actions:

- **Refer to peer / partner.** The lead is a fit for a different vendor. Referring cleanly builds partnership goodwill and often reciprocal referrals; the AE spends 10 minutes on a referral instead of nine weeks on a doomed cycle. Requires a maintained partner list — mod-108 develops the partner-relationship discipline.
- **Downgrade to a lower ACV tier.** Requires a productised lower tier — mod-106 pricing and packaging. Downgrade at SEED is often not available; it becomes available when the lower tier is deliberately built.
- **Add to segment waitlist / nurture.** For segments where the "not now" is a whole-product gap or an adjacent-motion, the lead goes on a segment-specific nurture list. When the unlock condition trips (SSO ships, first enterprise motion staffed), the entire waitlist re-opens as warm leads. This is a real revenue stream — not a graveyard — if the founder actually monitors and unlocks.
- **Close-lost with reason code.** For segments where no plausible unlock condition exists (the beachhead-adjacency risk is structural, not temporary), close-lost cleanly with the specific reason. The lead is not on any list; the AE moves on.

The meanwhile action is the concrete operational bridge between the beachhead-discipline decision (say no) and the individual-deal execution (do this instead). Without it, the "not now" section is aspirational.

### The unlock condition — writing "not now" as a conditional, not a forever-no

The reason the "not now" section says *not now* and not *no* is that most of the excluded segments are legitimately future-serveable — they just are not the current SEED-stage battle. Writing the unlock condition captures three things:

- The *specific* thing that would change the calculation. Not "when we grow"; "when we have SOC 2 Type II, an enterprise motion staffed with at least one enterprise AE, and a healthcare-vertical partner." A specific unlock is auditable — six months from now the founder can look at the list and say yes / no to whether it has tripped.
- The *implicit* prioritisation of the roadmap. If four not-now segments all unlock on the same triggering event (say, SOC 2 Type II), that is a strong signal to prioritise the SOC 2 investment. The list becomes a demand-signal for the roadmap.
- The *conservatism* on the yes side. If the unlock condition is far off ("18-month enterprise motion investment"), the founder is committing to the beachhead for 18 months. Naming that is honest and clarifying.

A "not now" without an unlock condition is a permanent no; that is a legitimate choice for some segments (segments that are structurally outside the product's addressable universe) but should be an explicit "structural no — do not revisit" rather than a hidden "we forgot to write the unlock."

### How the "not now" list feeds mod-107 (motion design) and mod-108 (channel selection)

The three said-no-to segments in mod-104 are the working input for the segment-motion-design and channel-selection work of mod-107 and mod-108. Specifically:

- **mod-107 sales-motion design** re-evaluates each not-now segment against motion fit: if we want to serve the enterprise segment eventually, what motion is required (SDR-AE, field-sales, enterprise MEDDPICC) and how does it differ from the mid-market beachhead motion? What motion is required for the horizontal expansion? For the smaller-ACV segment? The not-now list is the map of *which motions we will need to design when* — sequenced by when each unlock condition is expected to trip.
- **mod-108 demand-generation and channels** re-evaluates each not-now segment against channel fit: the fintech segment's demand-gen channels (fintech-specific publications, ISF conferences) are different from the devtools segment's; the enterprise segment reaches its buyers via analyst reports and enterprise procurement events, which the mid-market motion never touches. The not-now list is the map of *which channels we will need to invest in later*.

The not-now list is thus not a document that sits inert. It is *the input document* to two later modules. Chapter 7's shipping artifact makes this hand-off explicit: the ICP document names the not-now segments; mod-107 and mod-108 exercises pick them up.

### The quarterly "should we still be saying no?" review

The founder's operating cadence includes a quarterly review of the not-now list. The three questions:

1. **Has any unlock condition tripped?** Has SOC 2 shipped? Has the enterprise AE been hired? If yes, the segment is no longer "not now"; the founder decides whether to re-open it. This may become the natural moment the beachhead expands to the second bowling pin (mod-103 Chapter 5).
2. **Has the beachhead been won?** Are the beachhead-segment reference-pattern conditions satisfied — 5-10 named customers, published case studies, category-specific analyst coverage, presence in the segment's peer community (per mod-103 Chapter 5)? If yes, the founder is now in the chasm-crossing conversation and the not-now segments become candidate second-row pins.
3. **Has anything about the segments' shape changed?** A regulatory change, a competitor bankruptcy, a technology shift may make a previously not-now segment newly attractive or a previously beachhead-segment newly unfavourable. Quarterly re-check.

Without the quarterly cadence, the not-now list either ossifies (segments stay excluded years after they should have been re-opened) or dissolves (the AE takes deals from not-now segments case by case). The quarterly review is what keeps the list a living artifact.

### The disqualifier + not-now interaction

Chapter 3's disqualifier D4 (beachhead-scope) is the deal-level enforcement of the not-now list. The two artifacts work together:

- **The not-now list** names *which segments* are excluded.
- **Disqualifier D4** triggers *automatically* on any lead in a not-now segment (from the firmographic filter check).
- **The meanwhile action** in the not-now list tells the AE what to do with the disqualified lead — refer, downgrade, waitlist, close-lost.

An ICP artifact that has D4 but no not-now list has an enforcement mechanism with no criteria; an ICP artifact that has a not-now list but no D4 has criteria with no enforcement. Both are required; both live on the same one-page scorecard.

## Concrete example — Loomly's not-now list

Extending Loomly's ICP (Chapters 1-5) with the not-now section:

### Yes-segment (the beachhead)

Mid-market (50-150-engineer) B2B SaaS **devtools / observability / security-tooling** companies using GitHub, distributed, with an engineering leader owning a throughput / cycle-time metric.

### Not-now segments

**Not-now segment 1: Enterprise (> 500 engineers) B2B SaaS**
- What it looks like: 500-5000 engineers; enterprise procurement (RFP > $25k); required SSO / SCIM; often BYOC or on-prem deployment; multi-year contracts.
- Why not now: whole-product gap (no SSO / SCIM / audit logs / on-prem deployment); wrong motion (requires enterprise MEDDPICC, not mid-market inside-sales); wrong AE profile.
- Unlock condition: SSO + SCIM + audit logs shipped; first enterprise AE hired; at least three mid-market Series-A pilot customers referenceable at the 300-500-engineer size (the natural expansion sequence).
- Meanwhile: refer inbound to Enterprise Waitlist (email capture + quarterly "we are not yet ready" update); outbound: no cold outreach to enterprise until unlock.
- Revisit trigger: monitored quarterly by founder; automatic trigger when unlock condition trips.

**Not-now segment 2: B2B SaaS in non-devtools verticals (fintech, healthtech, martech)**
- What it looks like: same firmographic (50-150 eng, distributed, GitHub) but industry is fintech / healthtech / martech / adtech.
- Why not now: reference pattern from devtools wins does not carry to fintech (different peer community, different conferences, different Slack channels); demand-gen channels are different (mod-108); beachhead concentration requires all sales energy on devtools.
- Unlock condition: 5-10 devtools reference customers established; devtools case studies published; founder is starting to see natural inbound from adjacent verticals without effort.
- Meanwhile: refer to nurture (marketing-automated content, quarterly personal check-in from founder); no active AE selling.
- Revisit trigger: monitored quarterly; unlock triggers a mod-107 motion-design re-evaluation for the second-pin segment.

**Not-now segment 3: Individual OSS maintainers and hobbyist / bootstrapped teams (< 10 engineers)**
- What it looks like: OSS project maintainers; bootstrapped indie hackers; small teams < 10 engineers.
- Why not now: ACV too low to sustain sales cycle; no reference-pattern carry to mid-market beachhead; buyer psychology fundamentally different (individual not organisational).
- Unlock condition: PLG self-serve tier productised (mod-106 packaging + mod-107 motion-design work); when a self-serve tier exists, this segment can be served via product, not sales.
- Meanwhile: waitlist for self-serve tier; refer inbound demo requests to the waitlist with a "we're building a self-serve tier for teams your size, join here" message.
- Revisit trigger: at the point self-serve tier ships (targeted for month N).

### The hand-off

- To **mod-107 sales-motion design**: three motions to design over time — enterprise MEDDPICC (unlock 1), non-devtools vertical extension of the mid-market motion (unlock 2), and PLG self-serve (unlock 3). The not-now list is the sequencing input for the motion-design pipeline.
- To **mod-108 channel-selection**: at least three channel-stack changes correspondingly — enterprise analyst / conference / partner channels; fintech-vertical publications and communities; PLG-native distribution (SEO, product hunt, developer-community organic).

Neither of those mod-107 / mod-108 activities are in flight during the current beachhead-only quarter. They are the map for the next 12-24 months.

## Common failure patterns

- **Beachhead written in mod-103 but not enforced in mod-104.** The founder committed to the beachhead in the positioning workshop, but the ICP artifact does not have a not-now section, and the AE takes deals from all segments. The beachhead commitment dissolves silently.
- **"Not now" without unlock condition.** Vague "we might revisit" language that produces no auditable trigger. The segment stays excluded forever (bad — lost future revenue) or is re-opened arbitrarily (bad — beachhead dissolved). Named unlock conditions or explicit structural-no verdicts.
- **"Not now" without meanwhile action.** The AE sees the excluded lead and improvises — usually by hedging and following up for two months anyway. Every not-now segment needs a specific meanwhile: refer / downgrade / waitlist / close-lost.
- **Waitlist that no one monitors.** Prospects go on the enterprise waitlist and never hear from Loomly again. When the unlock condition trips, the waitlist is stale and the leads have adopted competitors. Someone must own the waitlist and email it at unlock.
- **Founder deal-by-deal exceptions.** An out-of-beachhead deal comes in that "seems too good"; the founder overrides the not-now list to work it. Rarely closes; always erodes the beachhead commitment. The right posture: refer or waitlist even the seductive deal, unless the specific deal is exactly the *unlock trigger* the segment was waiting for.
- **Not-now list that never gets reviewed.** The founder set it once and never revisited. Segments that should have unlocked at month 12 are still marked "not now" at month 24. Quarterly cadence, on the founder's calendar.
- **Too many not-now segments.** The founder writes 12 not-now segments. The AE cannot remember them; the meanwhile actions are all improvised. Three to five is the working range; if the list is longer, the beachhead was probably too narrow and needs re-scoping.
- **Not-now list with no roadmap connection.** The unlock conditions imply a roadmap ("we need SSO, SCIM, on-prem"); if the roadmap does not reflect the not-now list, the unlocks will never trip. mod-107 / mod-108 / product roadmap all read the not-now list; if they don't, the list is unactionable.
- **Beachhead-adjacency risk under-weighted.** The founder picks a not-now segment where the *distance* from the beachhead is small ("fintech is basically like devtools") and permits deals from it. Peer community differs; reference pattern doesn't carry; the beachhead commitment is diluted. Adjacency requires the mod-103 Chapter 5 test — is the reference pattern transferable — and often the answer is no even when the firmographic distance is small.

## Summary

- The **beachhead choice made in mod-103** requires an **enforcement instrument at the mod-104 ICP layer**. Chapter 3's disqualifier D4 is the deal-level lever; this chapter's not-now list is the segment-level lever.
- The **not-now section of the ICP** names the three (or 2-5) segments the founder has explicitly committed to not-serve during the current beachhead. Format: what it looks like, why not now, unlock condition, meanwhile action, revisit trigger.
- The **four common not-now categories**: the obvious adjacent segment (peer-community mismatch), the aspirationally larger segment (usually enterprise), the emotionally sympathetic smaller segment (usually OSS / individual), the horizontal-adjacent product play (positioning, not just segmentation).
- Every not-now segment carries a **specific unlock condition** — the triggering event that would let the founder re-open the segment. Unlocks are auditable; "we'll see" is not.
- Every not-now segment carries a **specific meanwhile action**: refer, downgrade, waitlist, or close-lost. Without the meanwhile, the AE improvises and re-drifts into serving the segment.
- **The not-now list is the working input for mod-107 (motion design) and mod-108 (channel selection)** — the map of which sales motions and demand-gen channels will need to be built when the beachhead has been won and the next pin opens.
- The **quarterly review** — "has any unlock tripped; has the beachhead been won; has any segment's shape changed" — is what keeps the not-now list a living artifact rather than a shelfware document.
- **Deal-by-deal founder exceptions** to the not-now list are the single most-common way the beachhead commitment dissolves; the whole point of the artifact is to make the commitment binding at the deal level.
- Chapter 7 develops the **shipping artifact** — the one-page ICP scorecard that carries the qualifying criteria, the disqualification checklist, the buyer / user / champion personas, the three-axis routing, and the not-now list — plus the ICP-drift diagnosis that catches the founder pattern of taking any customer that will pay.
