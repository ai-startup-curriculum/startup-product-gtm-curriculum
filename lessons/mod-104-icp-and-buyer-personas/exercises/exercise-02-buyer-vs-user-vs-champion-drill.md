# Exercise 02 — Buyer / User / Champion Persona Drill

**Estimated time:** 3 hours
**Chapter link:** [`04-buyer-user-champion-personas.md`](../04-buyer-user-champion-personas.md)
**Prerequisite:** Exercise 01 (ICP scorecard with persona placeholders); Chapter 4 read end-to-end

## Problem statement

Chapter 4 named the three personas — buyer (signs), user (touches product), champion (advocates internally) — and specified that at enterprise ACV they are always three different people, each with a different value story, each with a specific discovery question the AE uses to identify who fills the role at a specific target company. Chapter 4 also named the *champion-without-authority trap* — the AE spends a cycle with a friendly champion, treats them as the buyer, and dies at "let me take this back to my VP" at week 8.

This exercise trains you to (a) author the three personas for your startup with the specific discovery questions and value stories the AE runs in real time, (b) map real personas at three named target accounts (turning generic persona descriptions into specific mapped humans), and (c) role-play a discovery-call sequence that surfaces the persona split at a specific account.

The output is the persona section of the ICP scorecard (Chapter 7's format) plus three worked account-level persona maps — the artifact-and-application pair that turns the abstract persona split into an operating instrument.

## Requirements

Deliver a folder `exercise-02/` with:

- `part-a-persona-authoring.md` — the three personas fully authored.
- `part-b-account-mapping.md` — three named accounts with persona maps.
- `part-c-role-play-transcript.md` — a scripted discovery-call sequence that surfaces personas.

### Part A — Author the three personas (75 min)

Deliver `part-a-persona-authoring.md`. Use the same startup you picked in Exercise 01. For each persona, produce the full seven fields (per Chapter 4's ICP-artifact template):

**Buyer persona**
- **Titles.** 2-4 typical titles. Be specific to the ACV band and buying-motion posture your ICP targets. "Executive leadership" is not a title; "VP Engineering, sometimes CTO at < 100-engineer companies" is.
- **Reports to.** Who does the buyer answer to? The buyer's boss is often the ultimate over-ruler on a big-ticket deal.
- **Owns metric.** The specific board- or exec-facing metric the buyer is accountable for. This is the metric your value story must move.
- **Budget authority.** ACV band at which the buyer can sign without escalation; the escalation path if the ACV exceeds it.
- **Discovery question.** The exact 15-20-word question the AE asks in the first call to identify who at the target company is the buyer.
- **Value story.** 2-3 bullets tuned to the buyer's frame — business outcome, ROI, defensibility to their boss.
- **Common objections.** 2-3 objections the buyer typically raises; the AE's counter for each.

**Champion persona**
- **Titles.** 2-4 titles typical for champions in your ICP.
- **Reports to.** Champion's manager — often the buyer.
- **Motivation.** Why does the champion advocate? What's the personal stake — team-level metric in their perf review, career-relevant narrative, credit for the initiative?
- **Political capital.** What can the champion actually do inside the org — get a meeting on the buyer's calendar in a week, wrangle security review, run a pilot without extra approvals? How much political capital is required for the deal to close, and does this champion have that much?
- **Discovery question.** The 15-20-word question that identifies whether the person on the call is the champion.
- **Value story.** 2-3 bullets: team outcome + personal credit.
- **Common objections.** 2-3: risk of championing something that fails; time cost of running an evaluation; competing priorities.

**User persona**
- **Titles.** 2-4 titles typical for users.
- **Reports to.** Users usually report to champions or one level below.
- **Uses product.** Frequency and depth of usage — daily / weekly? Actively (opens dashboard) or passively (receives notifications)?
- **Discovery question.** The 15-20-word question that identifies who on the target team will be the actual user.
- **Value story.** 2-3 bullets: day-to-day workflow benefit.
- **Common objections.** 2-3: adoption cost, existing-tool substitution, "another notification."

Additionally, at the top of Part A, state:

- **ACV band** you're designing for ($X to $Y).
- **How much do the three personas collapse at this ACV band?** (E.g., "buyer and champion usually collapse; user distinct" for mid-market; "all three distinct" for enterprise; "all three same person" for PLG.)

### Part B — Map personas at three named accounts (45 min)

Deliver `part-b-account-mapping.md`. Pick three real target accounts from your ICP — either accounts you've already engaged with or accounts you would target next quarter. For each account:

- **Account name + firmographic snapshot.** Company name, employee count, industry, funding stage — the Layer 1 fields from your Exercise 01 scorecard.
- **Buyer at this account.** Named person if you know them, or the title + reporting relationship if you don't. Source: LinkedIn, past correspondence, company org chart if public.
- **Champion at this account.** Named person or best guess by title, plus reasoning ("engineering manager most likely to champion because the tool most helps mid-team leads with cycle-time OKRs").
- **User at this account.** Titles and team locations.
- **Persona-collapse note.** Do any personas collapse at this specific account? (E.g., "This is a 60-engineer company where the VP Eng is also actively engineering — buyer, champion, and probably a heavy user all in one.")
- **Gap analysis.** Which persona do you not know yet? What's your plan to identify it?

Three accounts, three maps. If you cannot map the personas for three real accounts, you have not applied the ICP to real prospects — go back and pick three you can research.

### Part C — Discovery-call role-play (60 min)

Deliver `part-c-role-play-transcript.md`. Script a first-discovery-call transcript for one of the three accounts from Part B — 15-20 minutes of dialogue in written form. The transcript should demonstrate the persona-mapping discipline in action.

Structure:

- **Setup (2 min).** AE greets champion (assume the initial demo request came from a champion-level contact). Establishes context.
- **Persona-mapping sequence (8-10 min).** The AE asks the three persona-identification questions (buyer, user, champion — the champion is on the call so that identification is implicit). Show the AE's transitions between questions naturally; do not stack them as an interrogation.
- **Verify champion has political capital (3 min).** The AE surfaces whether the champion can actually shepherd the deal — "how would a purchase of this size typically get approved at your company?" — and detects the champion-without-authority signal or its absence.
- **Handoff / next steps (2 min).** The AE proposes the next step — a follow-up with the buyer, a pilot with the user team, or a champion-led internal deck. The specific next step depends on what the persona map surfaces.

For each AE turn in the transcript, add a footnote naming (a) which persona is being probed, (b) what specific signal the AE is listening for, (c) what the AE would do differently if the answer went a different way.

At the end of the transcript, write a one-paragraph diagnosis:

- What did the AE learn about the persona map at this account?
- What are the deal's risks based on the persona split (e.g., "champion is a senior engineer with no direct line to the VP Eng — deal will require external help getting to the buyer")?
- What is the next-call objective given what surfaced?

The point of the role-play is not to prove the AE is clever; it is to make the persona-mapping discipline muscle-memory. If the AE in your transcript ends the call without knowing who the buyer is, the transcript has failed the exercise — rewrite the sequence.

## Starter guidance

- **Author the buyer persona first, not last.** Founders default to writing the user persona because that's the persona whose pain the product solves. But the buyer is the persona the deal actually closes on; the buyer's value story is the one the AE has to be able to deliver crisply. Author the buyer first.
- **Personas are role-defined, not title-defined.** Do not lead with "VP Engineering is the buyer." Lead with "the person who signs the contract and owns the budget is the buyer; at our ACV band, that's usually the VP Engineering." The distinction seems pedantic and is load-bearing.
- **The value story per persona is a different value story, not a rephrasing.** The user cares about their day; the buyer cares about the business outcome; the champion cares about the team outcome plus personal credit. Same product, three different value framings. If your three value stories all read like the same story with different words, they're not tuned; try again.
- **The discovery question per persona has to be answerable in one sentence.** If the AE has to ask three follow-up questions to identify the buyer, the primary question is not sharp enough. Refine to a single-question surface.
- **For the account mapping (Part B), use LinkedIn.** Every named-persona field can be filled from LinkedIn + the company's public engineering blog + Crunchbase + your own past emails to the account. If you can't map a persona from public information, that's the AE's first-call objective, not a gap in the exercise.
- **For the role-play (Part C), record it out loud first.** Speak the AE's turns as if you were on the call. Where the phrasing sounds awkward or the transitions feel forced, edit. Written dialogue that reads well but sounds clunky spoken is not usable as an AE script.
- **Do not skip the "verify champion has political capital" section of the role-play.** This is where the champion-without-authority trap is caught. Every AE eventually has one deal die because they skipped this section on some particular call. Practice the muscle.
- **Match persona depth to ACV band.** If your ACV band is PLG ($15/month), do not force a three-persona split — collapse to one, note the collapse at the top of Part A, and simplify the artifact. The three-persona split is expensive; don't over-invest at low ACV.

Your submission is complete when:

- [ ] Part A authors all three personas with the full seven fields each (titles, reports-to, metric/motivation/usage, budget-authority/political-capital/frequency, discovery question, value story, common objections).
- [ ] Part A states the ACV band and the expected persona-collapse pattern at that band.
- [ ] The value stories are **distinct across personas** — the buyer story is not a re-worded version of the user story. Each is tuned to the audience's frame.
- [ ] Each discovery question is a specific 15-20-word question the AE can ask directly in a discovery call.
- [ ] Part B maps three named accounts with buyer, champion, user identified (or explicitly noted as unknown with a plan to identify).
- [ ] Part B includes a persona-collapse note per account (all three distinct? some collapsed? all one person?).
- [ ] Part C is a 15-20-minute scripted discovery-call transcript that demonstrates the persona-mapping discipline — the AE identifies buyer, champion, and user during the call.
- [ ] Every AE turn in the transcript has a footnote naming (a) persona being probed, (b) signal being listened for, (c) alternate-branch action.
- [ ] The role-play includes an explicit "verify champion has political capital" section — the champion-without-authority trap is instrumented.

## Common ways this exercise goes wrong

- **Three personas that are the same person in three roles.** If your buyer, champion, and user all have titles like "engineering leader," you may be at a PLG or SMB ACV band where the personas legitimately collapse — or you may be under-differentiating the roles. Test at your ACV band: is there a real signer, a real advocate, a real day-to-day user? If all three collapse, note it explicitly and simplify.
- **Value stories that are all the same story.** The buyer, champion, and user care about different things at different altitudes. If your three value stories differ only in wording, re-author from the audience's frame.
- **Persona defined by title only.** "VP Engineering is the buyer" is a starting point, not a definition. The role — who signs, who owns budget — is the definition; the titles are typical fillers of the role.
- **Champion pitched as "person we can convince most easily."** The champion is defined by their ability to advocate and shepherd internally, not by their friendliness. A friendly-but-powerless contact is not a champion; it's a false champion, and treating them as one is Chapter 4's central failure mode.
- **Discovery question that requires three follow-ups.** "Tell me about your buying process" is not a discovery question; it's a monologue-prompt. "Who owns the budget for tools in this category?" is a discovery question.
- **Role-play transcript that skips the champion-authority verification.** The AE and champion have a friendly conversation, the AE proposes a follow-up, no one has surfaced whether the champion can get the follow-up on the calendar. This is where every enterprise deal dies. Instrument the verification explicitly.
- **Role-play that reads well but doesn't sound like a call.** Written dialogue that's clean on the page is often unnatural when spoken. Speak the transcript out loud to catch stilted phrasing.
- **Account mapping (Part B) done for three fictional accounts.** The mapping exercise loses most of its value if the accounts are made up. Pick real ones — even accounts you've never contacted; the persona-mapping-from-LinkedIn skill is the transferable outcome.
- **Marketing personas smuggled in.** "Marketing Mary with two dogs, a Peloton, and a fear of vendor lock-in" is a demand-gen content brief input, not an ICP persona. Different artifact, different exercise. Keep them separate.
- **Persona section that inflates the scorecard past one page.** The full persona detail belongs in a linked supporting document. The one-pager gets the compressed version: titles, discovery question, one-line value framing. If Exercise 01's scorecard grew past one page after this exercise, compress the on-page persona section and put the depth in a linked doc.
- **No objection handling.** The value stories are complete; the objections are absent. Every persona has typical objections; without them, the AE is unarmed against the resistance the value story will produce. Author 2-3 per persona with counters.
