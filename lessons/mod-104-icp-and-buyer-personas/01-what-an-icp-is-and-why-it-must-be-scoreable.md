# What an ICP Is — and Why It Must Be Scoreable on the First Call

## Motivation

An Ideal Customer Profile ("ICP") is one of the most-invoked and least-operationalised artifacts in early-stage GTM. Founders write ICPs that read like a marketing paragraph — "mid-market B2B SaaS companies with modern engineering cultures" — hand the paragraph to their first sales hire, and are surprised when that hire quotes a 500-person retailer, a two-person consultancy, and a Series-C fintech in the same pipeline review, all "ICP." The paragraph does not qualify. It decorates.

The bar this module holds every ICP to is specific: **a first sales hire — no product-domain expertise, no founder-tacit-knowledge, no shared browser history with the CEO — should be able to run a 20-minute discovery call, tick items on a scorecard, and end the call with a defensible in-or-out call.** If the ICP artifact does not support that action, it is not an ICP; it is a wish. Every downstream discipline in the module — the firmographic + behavioural criteria (Chapter 2), the disqualification checklist (Chapter 3), the buyer / user / champion split (Chapter 4), the ICP-fit / ACV-fit / timing-fit routing (Chapter 5), the beachhead-narrowed commitment (Chapter 6), the shipping artifact (Chapter 7) — exists to lift a marketing paragraph to that operational bar.

The failure mode the module exists to catch: **the founder writes an ICP that everyone in the company nods at, and no one — least of all the first AE hire — can actually apply on a live call.** Result: pipeline that is 3× the size it should be, close rate half what it should be, sales-cycle length twice what it should be, and no one can name why.

## Core concepts

### The operating definition

An **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)** is a scoreable description of the *company-level* buyer that (a) has the pain the product solves acutely enough that inaction is more expensive than the switching cost, (b) can achieve the value the product delivers with the whole-product surface available today, (c) can pay the current ACV without a heroic budget conversation, and (d) will generate a reference pattern the segment's other buyers respond to.

Three words in that definition are load-bearing.

- **Scoreable** — every criterion in the ICP resolves to a yes / no / partial answer against a real prospect. "Modern engineering culture" is not scoreable; "uses GitHub as primary code host, deploys to production ≥ 1× per week, on-call rotation is engineer-owned" is scoreable.
- **Company-level** — the ICP is about the *organisation*, not the person in the seat. Persons live in the buyer / user / champion personas (Chapter 4). Mixing person-level attributes into the company-level ICP is the single most common authoring mistake and produces an artifact that no one can score against a Crunchbase lookup.
- **Today** — the ICP describes the company you can win *right now*, with the product you have *today*, at the ACV you can sell *today*. Aspirational ICPs — "we would be ideal for Fortune 500 chief-medical-officer buyers once we have HIPAA and SOC 2 Type II and a healthcare-vertical AE" — are not ICPs; they are roadmap items. Chapter 6's beachhead discipline is what keeps the today-vs-someday line drawn.

### The "first-call qualify in / out" bar

The single practical test for whether an artifact is an ICP or an aspiration: **can a first sales hire, who has been at the company for six weeks, use it to make a defensible in-or-out call at the end of a discovery conversation, without escalating to the founder?**

The test forces four properties into the artifact:

1. **Concrete criteria** — not adjectives. "Employees > 200" is concrete; "growth-stage company" is not.
2. **Bounded number of criteria** — the AE has to hold them in their head during a live call. Six to eight scoreable dimensions is the practical ceiling; a 40-criterion "ICP checklist" is not a first-call instrument, it is a research document.
3. **Named disqualifiers** — the criteria that, if answered wrongly, make the deal a no *regardless of anything else on the call*. Chapter 3 develops this half.
4. **A commit-to-a-verdict rule** — the AE has to leave the call with an in / out / needs-second-call verdict, not an open field of "keep in touch." The rule is what makes the ICP a working instrument instead of a hopeful filter.

The bar exists because the alternative — every deal escalates to the founder, the founder tacitly re-qualifies from personal knowledge no one else has — is what makes the sales motion un-scalable and pushes the first-hire ramp from three months to nine. mod-105 (Founder-Led Sales) and mod-110 (First Hires) treat the same problem from the hiring side; this module treats it from the artifact side.

### Where the ICP sits in the operating stack

The ICP is not the first artifact in the strategic stack — it is the operational compression of the upstream work.

- **mod-101 discovery report** — problem statement + top jobs + top forces + disqualifying customer signals. The *input*. If the discovery corpus produced no repeatable "who has this pain" pattern, the ICP has nothing to compress.
- **mod-102 PMF scorecard** — the Sean Ellis cohort you scored on the "very disappointed" question. The *empirical calibration*. The ≥ 40% cohort's shared characteristics are the strongest single input to the ICP's firmographic + behavioural criteria.
- **mod-103 Dunford Component 4 (best-for characteristics)** — the customer traits that predict maximum resonance with the *value proposition*. The *strategic frame*. Best-for is wider than the ICP; the ICP is a specific slice of the best-for space made scoreable.
- **mod-103 Chapter 5 (Chasm beachhead)** — the single narrow segment you commit to winning first. The *segment commitment*. The beachhead is the segment; the ICP is the scoreable filter *inside* the beachhead.

The vertical relationship:

```
mod-103 best-for characteristics (widest — the space)
  → mod-103 beachhead segment (narrower — the first-slice commitment)
    → mod-104 ICP (narrower still — the scoreable filter inside the beachhead)
      → mod-104 buyer/user/champion personas (person-level — the humans in the seat)
        → mod-105 discovery-call script (the questions that score the ICP)
```

Skipping any layer produces the pathology this module exists to catch. Skip best-for and the ICP is arbitrary. Skip the beachhead and the ICP tries to serve five segments at once. Skip the personas and the ICP scores companies but never surfaces the human who has to sign, use, and advocate. Skip the discovery-call script and the ICP is a static document instead of a live instrument.

### What an ICP is not

Vocabulary confusion is chronic in this area. The following are *not* ICPs, though the words are often used interchangeably.

- **TAM / SAM / SOM (market sizing).** Total / Serviceable / Serviceable-Obtainable Addressable Market are financial estimates for a fundraising deck or a board slide. They describe *how many companies exist that could theoretically buy*. An ICP describes *which companies we can win, in what order, with what product, at what ACV*. A TAM answers "how big is the opportunity"; an ICP answers "who do we call today."
- **Best-for characteristics (mod-103 Dunford Component 4).** Best-for names the *characteristics that predict resonance with the value proposition* — a strategic frame authored during positioning. The ICP is narrower, scoreable, and includes the operational disqualifiers a positioning document does not.
- **Beachhead segment (mod-103 Chapter 5).** The beachhead is a *segment commitment* — the one segment you will concentrate on to earn the reference pattern. The ICP is the *filter you apply inside the beachhead* to decide which specific companies in that segment to call this quarter.
- **Buyer persona.** The buyer persona is *person-level* — the individual who signs the contract. Chapter 4 develops it. Confusing the buyer persona with the ICP produces a scorecard that treats "the buyer has a technical background" as a firmographic criterion, which is a category error.
- **Firmographics** (as a stand-alone list). Firmographics are one *input* to an ICP — company size, industry, geography. An ICP that is *only* firmographic (Chapter 2 develops this) misses the behavioural criteria that separate strong fits from weak fits inside the same firmographic bucket.
- **Lead scoring model.** A lead scoring model is a numeric derivation *from* the ICP applied to a specific inbound / outbound lead (Chapter 5). It is downstream, not the same thing.

Every one of these is a legitimate artifact. None of them is the ICP.

### The three views the ICP has to serve

An ICP that only serves one internal audience will fail the others. A shippable ICP serves three views simultaneously:

1. **The first-call qualification view.** The AE, on a discovery call, uses the scorecard to make an in-or-out decision in real time. This is the view that governs the *shape* of the artifact — bounded, scoreable, disqualifier-anchored.
2. **The marketing / demand-gen view.** The demand-gen owner (mod-108) uses the ICP to decide which segments to target with outbound, which content topics to prioritise for inbound, which conferences to sponsor, which lists to buy. This view needs the firmographic criteria to be *listable* — expressible as filters against LinkedIn Sales Navigator, ZoomInfo, Apollo, or an intent-data provider.
3. **The product / roadmap view.** The product owner uses the ICP to decide which features to prioritise, which integrations to build, which enterprise-only surfaces (SSO, SCIM, audit logs, VPC deployment) are on the critical path. If the ICP includes "companies with strict SSO enforcement," the SSO integration is a roadmap item; if it doesn't, SSO is a maybe-later.

An ICP that reads beautifully but cannot be filtered on LinkedIn Sales Navigator has failed view (2). An ICP that filters cleanly but does not name the whole-product surface has failed view (3). An ICP that names everything but cannot be scored on a live call has failed view (1). Chapter 7's artifact discipline is what makes a single document serve all three.

### The stage-specific bar — early SEED-stage ICPs are narrower than late-stage ICPs

The ICP a startup writes at SEED is *narrower* than the one it will run against at Series-B, and the shape of the narrowing is deliberate.

- **SEED / PRE-SEED ICP:** the smallest slice of the beachhead segment that (a) has the pain acutely, (b) can be served by the product's *current* whole-product surface, (c) will pay the *current* ACV, and (d) will produce a reference pattern the *next* slice responds to. The point is *concentration* — 30-50 target accounts a founder can name by hand, not a TAM.
- **Series-A ICP:** wider — the second and third bowling pins (mod-103 Chapter 5) are now inside the ICP because the reference pattern from the first pin is strong enough to open them. The scorecard grows a section that scores fit against the *expanded* segment.
- **Series-B and beyond:** wider still — the ICP now spans multiple segments and often includes multiple product SKUs. The scorecard becomes a segmentation model rather than a single filter.

The failure mode this chapter primes: **the founder writes the Series-A or Series-B ICP at SEED**, tries to serve five segments simultaneously, and produces an artifact that qualifies nothing in and disqualifies nothing out. Chapter 6 (beachhead) is the narrowing discipline; this chapter names it as the operating bar.

## Concrete example — Loomly's SEED ICP against three near-miss variants

Return to Loomly, the fictional PR-nudge tool from the mod-103 examples. mod-103 selected Positioning A ("code-review nudge tool for engineering managers") and the mid-market B2B SaaS devtools/observability segment as the beachhead.

The **SEED ICP** compressed against that beachhead:

- 50-150 engineers
- B2B SaaS, devtools or observability sub-vertical
- Distributed / async-first working model
- Using GitHub as primary code host (not GitLab / Bitbucket / Perforce)
- Engineering manager has an explicit "engineering throughput" or "cycle time" KPI they report to their VP or CEO
- ≥ 1 engineering manager reporting into a VP Eng, not directly into founder

Six criteria. Every one is scoreable on a first call by a first AE hire: the first four are filterable on LinkedIn Sales Navigator; the last two surface in a five-minute discovery conversation.

Three near-miss variants that would break the ICP:

**Variant A — the "marketing paragraph" ICP.**
> Modern engineering organisations that value velocity and want to give their managers superpowers.

Fails all four properties. Not scoreable ("modern," "value velocity"). Not bounded. No disqualifiers. No commit-to-verdict rule. This is decoration; the AE cannot apply it.

**Variant B — the "expanded aspirational" ICP.**
> B2B SaaS or fintech or healthtech companies, 50-1000 engineers, using GitHub or GitLab, distributed or hybrid, with a mid-level or senior engineering leader who cares about developer productivity.

Scoreable but not bounded. Serves too many segments — devtools mid-market, healthtech enterprise, fintech Series-B — with three different whole-product gaps (SSO for enterprise, SOC 2 Type II for fintech, HIPAA for healthtech). This is a Series-A / B ICP written at SEED. Chapter 6's beachhead discipline rejects it.

**Variant C — the "founder-in-the-room" ICP.**
> Engineering managers at 50-150-engineer devtools companies who are frustrated with their current process, technically curious, willing to try new tools, and have influence with their VP.

Person-level attributes ("frustrated," "technically curious") smuggled into the company-level filter. These are buyer *persona* attributes (Chapter 4), not ICP criteria. And "frustrated" is not scoreable on a first call from a Crunchbase lookup; it takes 20 minutes of discovery conversation to surface. Confuses the ICP with the persona.

The disciplined ICP — six firmographic + behavioural criteria at the company level, with the persona attributes lifted to the buyer / user / champion sections — is the one the first AE can carry. The three variants are all what the module trains the founder to *reject*.

## Common failure patterns

- **The marketing-paragraph ICP.** Reads well; qualifies nothing. If the AE cannot apply it on a live call, it is decoration, not an operating instrument.
- **The "we could serve them all" ICP.** Founder refuses to narrow because "we could be great for fintech too." Chapter 6 (beachhead) rejects this. At SEED, one segment; the others go on the not-now list.
- **Person-level attributes smuggled into the company-level ICP.** "Growth-mindset engineering leaders" is a persona attribute, not a firmographic one. It does not filter a target list; it does not survive the LinkedIn-Sales-Navigator test (view 2 in the three-views rubric). Lift persona attributes to Chapter 4's buyer / user / champion sections.
- **Aspirational ICP that requires unshipped product.** "Fortune 500 healthcare CIOs" is an ICP the current product cannot serve because HIPAA + SOC 2 Type II are not shipped. Roadmap item, not ICP.
- **ICP with no disqualifiers.** An ICP that only names who to say yes to, never who to say no to, produces a pipeline where the AE hedges every deal to "keep in touch." Chapter 3 develops the disqualifier discipline.
- **ICP as a marketing artifact instead of an operating one.** The ICP ships to marketing as copy for the "who we serve" page; the sales team gets a different, unwritten version from the founder. Two ICPs means no ICP.
- **No stage-specific narrowing.** The startup ships the same ICP at SEED that it will run at Series-B. The SEED version has to be dramatically narrower — concentration is the whole point.
- **ICP authored by committee.** The final ICP smooths every specific edge to consensus. Like positioning (mod-103), the ICP is a founder / CEO decision, pressure-tested by the team, not a workshop output.

## Summary

- An **Ideal Customer Profile** is a *scoreable*, *company-level*, *today*-tense description of the buyer the product can win with the current whole-product surface at the current ACV.
- The operating bar is the **first-call qualify in / out test**: a first sales hire, six weeks in, uses the scorecard on a discovery call to make a defensible in-or-out call *without escalating to the founder*. If the artifact does not support that action, it is not an ICP.
- The ICP has to serve **three internal views simultaneously**: first-call qualification (AE), demand-gen filter (marketing / mod-108), and roadmap gate (product). An artifact that only serves one has failed the other two.
- The ICP is **not** the TAM, the best-for characteristics, the beachhead segment, the buyer persona, or a lead-scoring model. It is downstream of the first three and upstream of the last two.
- SEED-stage ICPs are **narrower** than later-stage ICPs — 30-50 nameable target accounts inside the beachhead, not a TAM slice. Writing the Series-A ICP at SEED is the module's most common founder failure.
- Chapters 2-7 build the ICP artifact end-to-end: firmographic + behavioural criteria (2), disqualification checklist (3), buyer / user / champion personas (4), ICP-fit + ACV-fit + timing-fit routing (5), beachhead-narrowed commitment (6), one-page shipping artifact + drift diagnosis (7).
